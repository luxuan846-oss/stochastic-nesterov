                                           algorithms. We also introduce a unified approximate error condition for delayed inexact
                                           oracles, which can cover various practical scenarios.

This problem is fundamental in scientific computing and related fields such as optimization,
machine learning, engineering, probability and statistics, and economics, see, e.g., [2, 18, 27].
   It is well-known (e.g., [13]) that if we define G := I − F , where I is the identity mapping,
then (FP) can be reformulated equivalently into the following root-finding problem of G:

                               Find x⋆ ∈ Rp such that: Gx⋆ = 0,                               (CE)

where G is 12 -co-coercive, i.e., ⟨Gx − Gy, x − y⟩ ≥ 12 ∥Gx − Gy∥2 for all x, y ∈ Rp . Therefore,
approximating a fixed-point x⋆ of (FP) is equivalent to finding an approximate root of
(CE). For the sake of our presentation, we will treat the latter problem, i.e., (CE). While
we consider (CE) in a finite-dimensional space Rp , we believe that our results in this paper
can be extended to a Hilbert space with a little effort.

(b) Motivation. In parallel and distributed optimization methods, enforcing perfectly up-
to-date oracle evaluations such as [approximate] gradient or operator evaluations is often
prohibitively expensive, and in many cases, infeasible [4, 17, 74]. Both exact and approx-
imate synchronous oracle evaluations require global coordination across workers, memory
systems, and data partitions, which causes significant idle time and communication overhead
in heterogeneous computing environments. These limitations motivate the development of
algorithms that tolerate delayed or asynchronous inexact oracle information rather than
requiring workers to remain perfectly aligned to up-to-date oracle evaluations.
    Hitherto, most advanced asynchronous methods have been designed for minimization
problems; see, for example, [11, 17, 26, 37, 41, 47, 48, 53, 56, 68, 84, 85]. Such algorithms do not
directly accommodate modern applications in robust and distributionally robust optimiza-
tion, adversarial training, or reinforcement learning, which give rise to minimax or fixed-
point formulations. Only a limited number of works address more general models such as
(CE) and their special cases, including minimax problems; see [16, 25, 28, 30, 40, 66]. These
approaches largely extend classical asynchronous optimization schemes to fixed-point prob-
lems or monotone inclusions. However, these methods are often designed for a specific type
of asynchronous mechanism, such as asynchronous coordinate updates [16, 28, 40, 66] or in-
cremental aggregation [30]. In addition, these methods are non-accelerated, often require
restrictive assumptions (e.g., quasi-strong
                                             monotonicity), achieve sub-optimal convergence
rates (at most O 1/k or o 1/k rates), or have inefficient complexity bounds (e.g., quadratic
dependence on the maximum delay), see, e.g., [30, 40, 66]. For instance, [16, 28] studied asyn-
chronous fixed-point-type iterations for (FP) or (CE), but they are non-accelerated and
A Class of Accelerated Fixed-Point-Based Methods with Delayed Inexact Oracles                        3


only asymptotic convergence was established without explicit iteration or oracle complex-
ity bounds. Recently, [66] proposed an asynchronous block-coordinate fixed-point iteration
for (CE) that attains asymptotic convergence properties under Assumption 1, or a linear
convergence but requires a quasi-strong monotonicity of G, and its complexity could de-
pend quadratically on the maximum delay. This dependence was later improved to linear
in subsequent works, e.g., [37]. Recently, [30] developed asynchronous aggregated schemes
for solving (CE) extended to the finite-sum setting and obtained similar convergence and
complexity results using analogous block-coordinate updates and assumptions as in [66].
    The aim of this paper is to develop a unified accelerated fixed-point framework for (CE)
capable of handling delayed and inexact oracle evaluations and adaptable to a broad range
of asynchronous update mechanisms without quasi-strong monotonicity. Our approach inte-
grates Nesterov-type acceleration (different from previous works for (CE)), Krasnosel’skiǐ-
Mann iterations, and delayed inexact oracle information in a single scheme, while not using
the “quasi-strong monotonicity” of G as in [30, 66]. As a result, our framework yields sev-
eral improvements and new convergence properties compared to existing methods, even in
special cases such as smooth convex optimization and convex-concave minimax problems.

Algorithmic and analysis highlights. First, in the absence of inexactness, our AFP
scheme reduces to an exact deterministic version for solving (CE). Due to a different pa-
rameter update, this method is not identical to, but closely related to, Nesterov’s accelerated
scheme in [77] and the fast KM method in [19]. However, the convergence analyses in [19,
77] are not well suited for developing inexact variants of (CE). The difficulty arises from
bounding inner-product terms such as ⟨ek , y k+1 − y k ⟩ and ⟨ek , y k − x⋆ ⟩, where y k is the
iterate, x⋆ ∈ zer(G), and ek = G    e k − Gy k is the error between the inexact oracle G   e k and the
                k
true value Gy . Processing these terms is non-trivial in convergence analysis. This limitation
motivates us to develop a new scheme whose analysis avoids such terms entirely.
     Second, although our method may initially appear similar to Nesterov’s accelerated
scheme in [46, 62] for smooth convex minimization, the underlying analysis differs substan-

tially. In convex optimization, convergence analysis typically uses the objective residual
f (xk ) − f (x⋆ ) as part of the underlying Lyapunov function [12]. For general operator prob-
lems such as (CE), no such objective exists, and the analysis instead relies on constructing
an appropriate Lyapunov function – a nontrivial task in this context.
    Third, our method incorporates acceleration together with delayed inexact oracles, set-
ting it apart from existing inexact KM-type schemes such as [21, 29, 51]. It is also different
from recent non-accelerated paradigms in [28, 30, 66], where the analysis often uses a stan-
dard metric such as ∥xk −x⋆ ∥2 to construct a Lyapunov function. They often require stronger
assumptions for non-asymptotic rates, such as quasi-strong monotonicity [30, 66].
    Fourth, in the stochastic setting, our approach also differs from recent variance-reduced
fast KM methods [78, 79] due to the new algorithmic structure, convergence metrics, and
the generic delayed error approximation condition introduced in Definition 1.
    Finally, our framework is sufficiently general to accommodate additional variants beyond
the three settings studied in Section 4. It can also be applied to solve more general classes
of problems than (CE), such as monotone or co-hypomonotone inclusions, by applying ap-
propriate reformulation techniques as in [79] (see Remark 4).
(d) Related work and comparison. Since (FP) or its equivalent form (CE) is classical and
fundamental, its theory, numerical methods, and applications have been widely developed
for many decades, including the following monographs [13, 70, 39]. Here, we only discuss and
compare the most relevant work to our setting and methods studied in this paper.
(i) Fixed-point methods and its accelerated variants. Both the Banach-Picard (BP) iteration
and the Krasnosel’skiǐ-Mann (KM) scheme are classical approaches for approximating a
solution of (FP) or, equivalently, (CE). The BP iteration requires the operator F to be
contractive, whereas the KM scheme only assumes that F is nonexpansive, and therefore
applies to a broader class of algorithms — particularly those arising in monotone operator
theory such as proximal-point and splitting schemes; see, for example, [13, 36, 57, 71]. While
the BP method achieves a linear convergence              rate due to the contractivity of F , the KM
scheme guarantees at most O 1/k or o 1/k rates in terms of the squared residual norm
under mere nonexpansiveness, which is far from the O 1/k 2 -optimal rate [64].
    Boosting the convergence rates of classical fixed-point methods has become an attrac-
tive research topic in recent years. Both Nesterov’s acceleration and Halpern’s fixed-point
iteration have been extensively studied for solving (FP) and (CE); see, for example, [7, 10,
15, 20, 24, 34, 44, 52, 54, 64, 67, 72, 77, 80, 82, 86]. Depending on the assumptions
                                                                                     and parameter
update rules, these methods achieve convergence rates as fast as O 1/k 2 or even o 1/k 2 .
                                                                                                     

These rates are substantially improved over the non-accelerated counterparts by a factor of
1/k and often match the optimal rate up to a constant factor in many settings [52, 65].
    Hitherto, however, most accelerated algorithms of this type are deterministic and rely on
exact oracles. Only a limited number of works address inexact oracles, with most focusing on
stochastic oracles or randomized coordinate updates; see, e.g., [22, 23, 81, 78, 79]. As observed
in convex optimization [33], accelerated methods tend to be more sensitive to oracle errors
than their non-accelerated counterparts. In addition, most convergence analysis of inexact
methods relies on inexact oracles defined through the objective function. Unlike convex
optimization, here the absence of an objective function in (FP) makes the development of
Nesterov-type inexact accelerated fixed-point algorithms particularly challenging.
(ii) Optimization algorithms with delayed or asynchronous updates. Asynchronous algo-
rithms have been extensively developed in the literature, primarily within the domain of
convex optimization and numerical linear algebra. The seminal monograph [17] arguably
A Class of Accelerated Fixed-Point-Based Methods with Delayed Inexact Oracles              5


laid the theoretical foundation for parallel and distributed computation, covering both syn-
chronous and asynchronous algorithmic mechanisms. The recent surge of interest in solving
large-scale problems on parallel and distributed computing platforms has revived and greatly
expanded research on asynchronous methods. A major milestone in this renewed interest
is [68], introducing HOGWILD!, which established near-optimal convergence guarantees for
lock-free stochastic gradient descent. Subsequent theoretical analyses have typically demon-
strated convergence degradation proportional either to the maximum delay [1, 6, 38, 50, 56,
75] or to the average delay [11, 26]. The work of [37] refined this characterization by re-
placing the maximum delay with a threshold parameter and established a non-accelerated
O 1/k best-iterate convergence rate in expected optimality gap. The ASAGA algorithm
[48] achieved linear convergence for finite-sum, smooth, and strongly convex problems, with
explicit step-size and constant dependencies on the bounded overlap (delay) parameter,
matching the serial SAGA performance up to delay-dependent factors and allowing near-
linear speedups. Similarly, A2BCD [41], a Nesterov’s accelerated block-coordinate method,
attained linear convergence with optimal complexity under moderate delay conditions. Nev-
ertheless, extending this approach to accelerated fixed-point methods remains largely open.
(iii) Fixed-point methods with delayed or asynchronous updates. Unlike optimization, asyn-
chronous algorithms for fixed-point problems such as (FP) and generalized equations such
as (CE) remain comparatively underdeveloped. While most early methods were presented
in [17], convergence rates and complexity analysis similar to those in optimization remain
largely elusive. Remarkably, the work [66] systematically studied a class of asynchronous
coordinate schemes for (CE) based on the KM iteration, called ARock, allowing inconsis-
tent reads and finite maximal delays, and establishing almost sure convergence (and linear
rates under an additional quasi-strong monotonicity assumption) with delay-dependent step
sizes. Subsequent variants of ARock can be found, e.g., in [40, 42]. The work [30] extended
ARock’s idea and developed asynchronously incremental aggregated schemes for a finite-sum
setting of (CE) and obtained similar convergence results as [66] under analogous assump-
tions. For monotone inclusions, [28] proposed an asynchronous block-iterative primal-dual
splitting method with weak and strong asymptotic convergence guarantees. These methods,
however, are non-accelerated, highlighting the need for a general, non-asymptotic, and ac-
celerated framework capable of accommodating asynchrony in both nonexpansive mappings
and monotone equations or inclusions.
(e) Paper outline. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section 2 reviews the
necessary background and mathematical tools used in the sequel. Section 3 develops our
accelerated fixed-point framework with delayed inexact oracles and analyzes its convergence
under a general error approximation condition. Section 4 specifies our framework for three
concrete computing models, and establishes their convergence rates accordingly. Section 5
presents two numerical examples to verify our methods and theoretical results. For clarity
of presentation, we defer some technical proofs to the appendix.

(b) Filtration and expectations. We define by Fk the σ-algebra generated by all the
randomness induced by x0 , x1 , . . . , xk , up to the k-th iteration. This σ-algebra generates a

(c) Co-coercivity, Lipschitz continuity, and nonexpansiveness. For a single-valued
operator G : Rp → Rp , we say that G is β-co-coercive if there exists β ≥ 0 such that
⟨Gx − Gy, x − y⟩ ≥ β∥Gx − Gy∥2 for all x, y ∈ Rp ; we say that G is L-Lipschitz continuous
if there exists L ≥ 0 such that ∥Gx − Gy∥ ≤ L∥x − y∥ for all x, y ∈ dom(G). If L = 1,
then G is nonexpansive. If Gx = ∇f (x), the gradient of a convex function f , then the L-
Lipschitz continuity of ∇f is equivalent to the L1 -co-coercivity of ∇f due to the well-known
Baillon–Haddad theorem [13].
(d) Monotonicity and co-hypomonotonicity. For a possibly multivalued operator G :
Rp ⇒ Rp , we say that G is ρ-co-hypomonotone if ⟨wx − wy , x − y⟩ ≥ −ρ∥wx − wy ∥2 for
all (x, wx ), (y, wy ) ∈ gra(G) and ρ ≥ 0. If ρ = 0, then we say that G is monotone. A
co-hypomonotone operator is not necessarily monotone, see, e.g., [79] for concrete examples.

Remark 4 [Extension to generalized equations] We can apply our scheme (AFP) to
solve the following generalized equation (known as inclusion):

                          Find x⋆ ∈ Rp such that: 0 ∈ Gx⋆ + T x⋆ ,                       (GE)

where G is defined as in (CE), while T : Rp ⇒ Rp is a possibly multivalued mapping that is
ρ-co-hypomonotone. We sketch the main steps of this application as follows.
• First, we equivalently reformulate (GE) into (CE) using either a forward-backward split-
   ting (FBS) operator or a backward-forward splitting (BFS) operator as done in [79].
• Next, we develop a similar result as Corollary 1 in [79] to verify Assumption 1.
• Then, we apply (AFP) and its convergence theory to the resulting FBS or BFS equation.
• Finally, we convert the convergence results of this equation to the original problem (GE).
More generally, our framework (AFP) can be applied to solve any problem that can be
reformulated into the fixed-point problem (FP) of a nonexpansive operator F .

4.1 Accelerated Fixed-Point Method with Delayed Oracles
In this setting of (CE), we do not assume any structure on G and treat it as a universal
operator satisfying Assumption 1(ii) with β̄ = 0 and β > 0, i.e., G is β-co-coercive. This
setting is equivalent to finding a fixed-point of a nonexpansive operator F in (FP).
4.1.1 The computing mechanism
We consider a standard server-worker system outlined in Figure 1 with one central server
and multiple workers. The server maintains (i) the decision variables in a V-memory block
and (ii) the most recent operator value in a G-memory block. All workers have read access
to the shared data and can therefore evaluate the operator (though their speeds may differ).
(a) Protocol. The server initializes V-memory with y 0 and broadcasts the current iterate y k
whenever updated. Upon receiving y k , a worker computes Gy k using the shared data and,
when finished, attempts to commit the result to G-memory. A successful commit triggers the
server to form the next iterates xk+1 , y k+1 , z k+1 and atomically write them to V-memory.
(b) Atomic assumption. All reads/writes to G- and V-memory are atomic: a memory
region being written is locked (mutual exclusion), so every reader observes either the old
value or the fully written new value – never a mixture (no torn reads). In practice, such
atomicity can be enforced using some technical or systems techniques, such as a memory
lock or double-/dual-memory (double buffering) with an atomic swaps approach [66].

only through an unbiased stochastic oracle G(·, ξ) with bounded variance defined on a given
probability space. More specifically, we impose the following additional assumption on G:
Assumption 2 G in (CE) is equipped with an unbiased stochastic oracle G(·, ξ) such that

                              Gx = Eξ [G(x, ξ)],     ∀x ∈ dom(G).                              (41)

Moreover, there exists σ ≥ 0 such that Eξ [∥G(x, ξ) − Gx∥2 ] ≤ σ 2 for all x ∈ dom(G).
This assumption is standard in stochastic approximation methods, see, e.g., [59], and it has
been extended to different forms. Here, we simply use a simple version as in [59].
4.2.1 The computing mechanism
The scheme studied in this subsection is the same as (AFP), and the mechanism is very
similar to the mechanism studied in the previous subsection. However, unlike Subsection 4.1,
due to the inaccessibility to the full operator G, workers only return a stochastic estimator
of Gy k for any k ≥ 0 rather than the true value. This leads to the choice G  e k used in this
variant of (AFP) as an “delayed stochastic estimator”, i.e., a stochastic estimator of an
obsolete operator value Gy k−τk for some τk ∈ {0, 1, · · · , τ }.

5.2 Training neural networks via delayed inexact oracles
In this example, we evaluate different variants of our framework (AFP) on a simple training
task involving a shallow neural network. It is motivated by the following observations. First,
training large-scale machine learning models is typically carried out on parallel or distributed
systems equipped with multiple CPU or GPU processors. In such environments, the use
of delayed inexact oracles is unavoidable. Second, although the underlying optimization
problem of this task is nonconvex, the network architecture contains only a few hidden
layers, making its landscape relatively simple compared to deep learning models. Third,
several existing studies have shown that training neural networks using variants of Nesterov’s
momentum can improve performance, see, e.g., [76].
