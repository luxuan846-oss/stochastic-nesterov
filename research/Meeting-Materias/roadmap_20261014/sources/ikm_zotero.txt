                                                                                                    Abstract
                                                      We analyze a simple stochastic inertial Krasnosel’skii–Mann (iKM) method for finding a fixed point
                                                  of a nonexpansive operator in a real Hilbert space. Our method is obtained simply by adding two inertial
                                                  extrapolations to stochastic KM [Bravo and Cominetti, 2024], and it retains one call to a possibly biased
                                                  stochastic oracle per update and achieves sharp rates in both the stochastic and deterministic regimes.
                                                  Specifically, with our proposed parameter schedule, we prove the following last-iterate fixed-point residual
                                                  bound:                                                            
                                                                                           1    σ log K   BK log K
                                                                                       O     + √        +              ,
                                                                                           K        K         K
                                                  where K is√the horizon, σ is the noise level and BK is the accumulated root-mean-square bias. When
                                                  BK = O( K), this yields e     O(ϵ−2 ) sample complexity that matches, up to a logarithmic factor, the
                                                  stochastic-oracle lower bound given under the unbiased subclass of our model [Foster et al., 2019, The-
                                                  orem 2]. It also improves the best-known O(ϵ−4 ) random-iterate guarantee for stochastic KM [Bravo
                                                  and Cominetti, 2024, Corollary 5.4]. To our knowledge, this is the first single-loop method for general
                                                  nonexpansive fixed-point problems to attain this near-optimal sample complexity without variance re-

    In many modern applications dealing with large-scale or expectation-based settings, the exact evaluation
of applying the operator T may be unavailable or prohibitively costly, so sampling and approximate com-
putation naturally produce noisy and potentially biased responses [Bravo and Cominetti, 2024; Cui et al.,
2019]. Consequently, we focus on the stochastic case where the operator T is not available exactly: at each
query, the algorithm receives a noisy and possibly biased response. Given a target precision ϵ > 0, we study
how many such oracle responses are needed to make the last-iterate residual at most ϵ.
    Perhaps the most common approach to stochastic fixed-point iterations are obtained by replacing the
exact operator evaluations in classical deterministic schemes with stochastic oracle responses. These include
the Krasnosel’skii–Mann (KM) iteration [Mann, 1953; Krasnosel’skii, 1955] with update

                                        xk+1 = (1 − λk )xk + λk T xk ,                                     (1)

the anchored iteration of Halpern [1967] with update

                                        xk+1 = (1 − λk )x0 + λk T xk ,                                     (2)

and their accelerated variants [Maingé, 2008; Dong et al., 2018; Boţ and Nguyen, 2023; Park and Ryu,
2022]. Some of these schemes attain the optimal rate [Contreras and Cominetti, 2023; Park and Ryu,
2022] under the deterministic setting, see Section 1.2 for details. However, under stochastic evaluations,
existing results for general nonexpansive maps reveal a simplicity–complexity gap: simple methods have
substantially suboptimal sample complexity, whereas near-optimal guarantees require stronger assumptions
or more sophisticated algorithms. We next elaborate on this trade-off more quantitatively, as follows. To
begin, let us record a useful lower bound for the stochastic fixed-point problem studied here: given a target
precision ϵ, Foster et al. [2019, Theorem 2] established that at least Ω(ϵ−2 ) samples are needed, regardless
of algorithms in use.
   • Substantially sub-optimal rates for simple single-loop algorithms. For stochastic KM itera-
     tion, Bravo and Cominetti [2024, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 5.4] obtained sample-complexity bounds
     of O(ϵ−6 ) and O(ϵ−4 ) for the expected residual of the last iterate and a weighted random iterate,
     respectively. Cortild and Cartis [2026, Corollary 2.10] also obtained the O(ϵ−4 ) rate for one-sample
     stochastic KM. For stochastic Halpern iteration, Bravo and Contreras [2026, Corollary 3.5] and Pischke
     and Powell [2026, Section 5 and Example 5.1] gave last-iterate sample-complexity bounds of O(ϵ  e −5 ),
     using minibatches of size e

   • Sophisticated algorithm designs to accelerate convergence. For general fixed-point equations,
     VR-GHAL [Diakonikolas, 2026, Algorithm 3 and Corollary 3] reached O(ϵ   e −2 ) complexity under sam-
     plewise nonexpansiveness, but required an array of techniques including recursive variance reduction,
     same-seed difference queries, nested epochs, and large refresh batches. Nguyen-Trung and Tran-Dinh

     Under related cocoercive assumptions, the recursively variance-reduced Halpern methods of Cai et al.
     [2022] attained O(ϵ−3 ) complexity in the general regime.

   This simplicity–complexity gap motivates the following question:
            Can a simple single-loop method attain near-optimal sample complexity for general
                              nonexpansive stochastic fixed-point problems?

   Inspired by the general inertial Krasnosel’skii–Mann (iKM) iteration introduced by Dong et al. [2018,
Section 3.2, Eq. (18), p. 181], we study its one-sample stochastic counterpart, and with carefully chosen
parameter schedule, provide a positive answer to the above question.

1.1    Our contributions
We analyze a simple stochastic inertial Krasnosel’skii–Mann (iKM) method, which adds two inertial extrap-
olations to stochastic KM as follows:
                                 yk = xk + αk (xk − xk−1 ),
                                zk = xk + βk (xk − xk−1 ),
                              xk+1 = (1 − λk )yk + λk Tbk (zk ),    k = 1, . . . , K.

Here, αk ≥ 0 and βk ≥ 0 are the two inertial extrapolation coefficients associated with yk and zk , respectively,
λk ∈ (0, 1] is the relaxation parameter, and Tbk is a noisy, and possibly biased, evaluation of the fixed-point
operator T . This algorithm is simple with a single-loop structure, making it highly implementable in practice.
In particular, each update retains exactly one stochastic oracle call, consistent with stochastic KM.
   With carefully designed schedule, we establish simultaneous fixed-point-residual bounds for the last iterate
xK and the two corresponding inertial extrapolations yK , zK of iKM on the order of
                                                                    
                                           1     σ log K    BK log K
                                       O      + √         +             ,
                                           K         K          K

where K is the horizon, σ is the noise level and BK is the accumulated root-mean-square bias.
   • Near-optimal
       √              stochastic sample complexity. When the accumulated bias BK is controlled by
     O( K), iKM has e   O(ϵ−2 ) sample complexity, matching the lower bound in [Foster et al., 2019, Theo-
     rem 2] for unbiased oracles up to one logarithmic factor, and significantly improving the best-known rate
     O(ϵ−4 ) of stochastic KM, which holds for a weaker random-iterate convergence [Bravo and Cominetti,
     2024, Corollary 5.4].

   • Optimal deterministic specialization. In the special case where the oracle is exact, namely Tbk = T
     for all k with σ = 0 and BK = 0, iKM attains a faster O(K −1 ) last-iterate rate, which is worst-case
     optimal for deterministic nonexpansive fixed-point methods [Park and Ryu, 2022, Theorem 4.6], and
     strictly improves the sharp Θ(K −1/2 ) order of classical KM [Davis and Yin, 2016].
    To the best of our knowledge, our work provides the first single-loop method for general nonexpansive
fixed-point problems to attain the near-optimal sample complexity for the stochastic setting, without so-
phisticated algorithmic operations such as variance reduction or batching. The same algorithm also achieves
optimality in the deterministic setting, therefore smoothly interpolating both regimes.

1.2    Related work

Classical deterministic fixed-point methods. Under exact evaluations, the classical KM iteration
has a sharp O(K −1/2 ) worst-case last-iterate residual rate for general nonexpansive operators [Cominetti
et al., 2014; Bravo and Cominetti, 2018]. The anchored Halpern iteration can attain the faster O(K −1 )
rate, with tight guarantees in Hilbert and general normed spaces [Lieder, 2021; Contreras and Cominetti,
2023]; this order is worst-case optimal in the deterministic fixed-point oracle model [Park and Ryu, 2022,
Theorem 4.6]. Related refinements include rates for operator-splitting schemes [Davis and Yin, 2016] and, in
finite dimensions, continuous families of methods with the same exact optimal guarantee [Yoon et al., 2024].
These results assume exact evaluations and do not quantify the effect of stochastic oracle errors.
Inertial and accelerated KM methods. We consider the two-extrapolation general iKM iteration orig-
inated from Dong et al. [2018, Section 3.2, Eq. (18), p. 181], which recovers the earlier common-extrapolation
scheme of Maingé [2008] when its two extrapolation coefficients are equal. Prior work establishes determin-
istic convergence for common- and two-extrapolation variants [Cui et al., 2019; Maulén et al., 2024; Cortild
and Peypouquet, 2025]. Besides, Boţ and Nguyen [2023]; Boţ et al. [2026] propose Fast KM - an accelerated
version of KM with optimal O(K −1 ) deterministic last-iterate rate. Appendix A shows that the Fast-KM
iteration can be seen as deterministic iKM under a particular hyperparameter choice. These analyses concern
exact evaluations or summable perturbations and do not account for stochastic-oracle complexity.

Stochastic fixed-point methods. Existing direct stochastic KM and Halpern guarantees fall short of
near-optimal sample complexity: available one-sample KM bounds are O(ϵ−4 ) or worse [Bravo and Cominetti,
2024; Cortild and Cartis, 2026], while minibatched analyses rely on growing batches [Iiduka, 2026; Bravo
and Contreras, 2026; Pischke and Powell, 2026]. Near-optimal rates instead require additional structure or
machinery. VR-GHAL [Diakonikolas, 2026, Algorithm 3 and Corollary 3] reaches e      O(ϵ−2 ) under sample-wise
nonexpansiveness using recursive variance reduction, which requires a stronger stochastic oracle supporting
same-seed difference queries. The restarted variance-reduced Halpern method of Cai et al. [2022] achieves
O(ϵ−2 log(1/ϵ)) only under an additional sharpness-type error bound, while variance-reduction-free S-Dual-
OHM [Yoon and Loizou, 2026] reaches O(ϵ    e −2 ) only under the stronger assumption that the residual operator
I − T is strongly monotone. The stochastic AFP method of Nguyen-Trung and Tran-Dinh [2025, Section 4.2
and Corollary 2] is single-loop but uses polynomially growing minibatches. A particularly relevant comparison
is the RAIN algorithm of Chen and Luo [2024], developed for stochastic minimax problems. After translating
to our setting, RAIN achieves O(log3 (1/ϵ)/ϵ2 ) oracle complexity instead of our O(log2 (1/ϵ)/ϵ2 ) rate. RAIN
employs a recursive regularization scheme similar to SGD3 [Allen-Zhu, 2018] (augmented with an extra
oracle call per iteration, inheriting both the log3 term and also a double-loop structure), while we take a
different single-loop, single-oracle call path based on iKM. Our method also accommodates general unknown
bias, does not require the distance D = dist(x1 , Fix T ) as an algorithmic input, and obtains a last-iterate
guarantee. To our best knowledge, no prior method combines one response per iteration, a single loop, and
near-optimal sample complexity for a general nonexpansive map under the stochastic oracle model considered
here.

2     Stochastic iKM under Nonexpansiveness
2.1    The stochastic iKM algorithm
Let T : H → H be nonexpansive, namely
                                    ∥T x − T y∥ ≤ ∥x − y∥ ,        ∀x, y ∈ H.                                (3)
We also assume its set of fixed points is non-empty, i.e.
                                       Fix T := {x ∈ H : T x = x} ̸= ∅.
Fix a horizon K ≥ 2. Let the initial points x0 , x1 ∈ H be arbitrary and deterministic. The stochastic iKM
scheme forms two inertial extrapolations and performs the oracle-based updates
                                 yk = xk + αk (xk − xk−1 ),
                                zk = xk + βk (xk − xk−1 ),                                                   (4)
                              xk+1 = (1 − λk )yk + λk Tbk (zk ),     k = 1, . . . , K.
Here, αk ≥ 0 and βk ≥ 0 are the two inertial extrapolation coefficients associated with yk and zk , respectively,
while λk ∈ (0, 1] is the relaxation parameter. Moreover, Tbk is a noisy realization of Tbk , evaluated by calling
some oracle model.
Special case: deterministic iKM. When the oracle is exact, so that bk = ξk = 0 almost surely and
Tbk (zk ) = T zk for every 1 ≤ k ≤ K, (4) reduces to the deterministic finite-horizon iKM scheme
                                  yk = xk + αk (xk − xk−1 ),
                                  zk = xk + βk (xk − xk−1 ),                                                 (5)
                                xk+1 = (1 − λk )yk + λk T zk ,      k = 1, . . . , K.

3    Conclusion
Our proposed iKM schedule achieves accelerated last-iterate convergence for iKM under nonexpansiveness
and standard stochastic-oracle assumptions. It reaches both near-optimal stochastic sample complexity and
the optimal deterministic last-iterate rate, using a simple single-loop update without variance reduction or
recursive regularization.
     These guarantees suggest two complementary directions for future work. First, it would be valuable to
develop and evaluate practical implementations of stochastic iKM in large-scale stochastic first-order and
operator-splitting methods. In particular, deep neural network training provide an important testbed of
how far the benefits extend beyond the present theory. Second, the fixed-point viewpoint makes it possible
to apply the stochastic iKM method for model-free reinforcement learning, where adapting the inertial
mechanism developed here may lead to simple, single-loop model-free algorithms that are more sample-
efficient.

A     Relation to Fast KM
Fast KM was introduced by Boţ and Nguyen [2023] as an accelerated method for finding fixed points of
nonexpansive operators. A recent generalization provides an explicit nonasymptotic O(K −1 ) fixed-point-
residual bound [Boţ et al., 2026, Remark 5], matching the optimal worst-case order for general nonexpansive
fixed-point problems [Park and Ryu, 2022, Theorem 4.6].
