                                                                                                                                            Abstract. In this paper, we introduce a novel extragradient method with anchor term gov-
                                                                                                                                       erned by general parameters. Our method is derived from an explicit discretization of a Tikhonov-
                                                                                                                                       regularized monotone flow in Hilbert space, which provides a theoretical foundation for analyzing
                                                                                                                                       its convergence properties. We establish strong convergence to specific points within the solution
                                                                                                                                       set, as well as convergence rates expressed in terms of the regularization parameters. Notably, our
                                                                                                                                       approach recovers the fast residual decay rate O(k - 1 ) for standard parameter choices. Numerical
                                                                                                                                       experiments highlight the competitiveness of the method and demonstrate how its flexible design
                                                                                                                                       enhances practical performance.

                                                                                                                                           Key words. monotone equations, Tikhonov regularization, strong convergence, convergence
                                                                                                                                       rates, extragradient method, Lyapunov analysis, explicit discretization

                                                                                                                                            1. Introduction. Tikhonov regularization has a long history in optimization.
                                                                                                                                       Originally introduced in the context of inverse problems as a powerful tool to stabilize
                                                                                                                                       ill-conditioned linear systems, it has quickly become popular as a simple mechanism
                                                                                                                                       to induce desirable properties in dynamical systems, such as strong convergence to
                                                                                                                                       a particular solution [14] and acceleration [7]. Let H be a real Hilbert space and
                                                                                                                                       consider the following problem:

                                                                                                                                       (1.1)                                                                                                               Find x \in  H : M (x) = 0 ,

                                                                                                                                       where M : H \rightarrow  H is a monotone and L-Lipschitz continuous operator. One might be
                                                                                                                                       tempted to approach (1.1) by considering the asymptotic behavior of the dynamical
                                                                                                                                       system

                                                                                                                                       (1.2)                                                                                                          \.x(t) + M (x(t)) = 0                                                 for t \geq  t0 ,

                                                                                                                                       and t0 > 0. However, trajectories of (1.2) are well known to only converge weakly and
                                                                                                                                       ergodically to a zero of M [4]. Tikhonov regularization [14] is one possible modification
                                                                                                                                       that allows us to overcome this issue. It consists in introducing a regularization
                                                                                                                                       function \varepsilon  : [t0 , +\infty ) \rightarrow  (0, +\infty ) and considering the regularized system

                                                                                                                                       (1.3)                                                                                     \.x(t) + M (x(t)) + \varepsilon (t) (x(t)  -  v) = 0                                                                                                                         for t \geq  t0 ,

                                                                                                                                       where v \in  H is the so-called anchor term. If \varepsilon  is constant, (1.3) is a special case of (1.2)
                                                                                                                                       with respect to the strongly monotone operator M\varepsilon  := M + \varepsilon (I  -  v), I : H \rightarrow  H being
                                                                                                                                       the identity operator. This dynamical system does indeed allow us to get a converging

                                                                                                                                                             1.3. Continuous time analysis. In this section, we briefly recall the contin-
                                                                                                                                       uous time analysis of the Tikhonov flow (1.3) with the careful adaptation recently
                                                                                                                                       introduced by Bo\c t and Nguyen in [7], which allows establishing a fast convergence
                                                                                                                                       rate for the residual. Despite our main focus being on numerical algorithms, it is
                                                                                                                                       important to include this analysis in this paper, since our discrete time approach is
                                                                                                                                       profoundly based on the continuous time approach, which emerges as an excellent
                                                                                                                                       assistant to:
                                                                                                                                                              1. Guide the convergence proof: Our Lyapunov analysis follows exactly the ar-
                                                                                                                                                                 guments in the continuous time approach, which is significantly simpler than
                                                                                                                                                                 its discrete time counterpart. We shall say that our approach is continuous
                                                                                                                                                                 time assisted.
                                                                                                                                                              2. Design converging algorithms: It will become clear in our analysis that certain
                                                                                                                                                                 design choices of our methods are dictated by the Lyapunov analysis itself.
                                                                                                                                                                 Specifically, these are often established to control undesired terms that do
                                                                                                                                                                 not appear in the continuous time approach.
                                                                                                                                       Let M : H \rightarrow  H be a monotone and L-Lipschitz continuous operator with zer M \not = \emptyset ,
                                                                                                                                       and consider the system (1.3). We assume that the regularization function \varepsilon  : [t0 ,
                                                                                                                                       +\infty ) \rightarrow  (0, +\infty ) is continuously differentiable. For every starting point x0 \in  H,
                                                                                                                                       the system admits a unique solution t \mapsto \rightarrow  x(t) of class C 1 with x(t0 ) = x0 , therefore
                                                                                                                                       t \mapsto \rightarrow  M (x(t)) is locally absolutely continuous and thus admits a derivative almost
                                                                                                                                       everywhere. We also observe that, without loss of generality, we shall assume v =
                                                                                                                                       0. The general case can then be retrieved with a suitable shift [6, subsection 2.3].
