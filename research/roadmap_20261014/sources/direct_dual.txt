Direct Acceleration of Stochastic Root-Finding
Without Variance Reduction and Regularization
TaeHo Yoon*
Nicolas Loizou*
Abstract
Acceleration for deterministic root-finding problems has been extensively studied in recent years; specifically, the anchor-based, or Halpern-type methods achieve optimal convergence rates with respect to the operator norm.
However, acceleration via these methods does not directly carry over to stochastic setting due to accumulation of errors, unless one enforces diminishing variance via increasing batch sizes or variance reduction techniques.
In this work, we show that another class of acceleration, namely the dual-anchor mechanism, extends to the stochastic setting without such error accumulation, in contrast to anchor-based algorithms.
Consequently, we cleanly achieve ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ complexity with iteration-independent batch size, without any variance reduction or double-loop recursive regularization, for stochastic root-finding (resp. fixed-point) problems with cocoercivity (resp. square-nonexpansivity) in expectation.
For strongly monotone operators, the same algorithm attains a sharper $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ complexity, nearly matching the lower bound in terms of $\epsilon$ -dependence.
1 Introduction
Root-finding problems for an operator ${\mathbb{F}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , stated as
$\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\text{find}}\quad{\mathbb{F}}(x)=0,$
(1)
generalize minimization problems and have been studied under distinct formulations including monotone inclusion [36, 42, 3, 43], minimax optimization [38, 19, 33, 13, 35, 31], equilibrium search [47, 17, 46], or fixed-point problems [24, 34, 21, 51, 45].
In the past few years, there have been significant developments in accelerated algorithms for reducing the squared operator/residual norm with the optimal $\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}={\mathcal{O}}(1/k^{2})$ rate [29, 16, 22, 40, 56, 27].
A recurring message from the optimization literature, however, is that deterministic acceleration is often brittle under oracle errors; in the stochastic setting where ${\mathbb{F}}(x)=\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)\right]$ and the algorithm uses $\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)$ , error may accumulate quickly with these accelerated methods and break their fast convergence from the noiseless setting.
Consequently, for stochastic optimization problems, taking advantage of the study of acceleration was not straightforward—it required decaying oracle inexactness, variance reduction, restart mechanisms, or stronger assumptions on the problem [15, 18, 28, 10].
Specifically for stochastic monotone inclusion problems, stochastic Halpern iteration and accelerated (anchored) extragradient type analyses suffered precisely from these issues [27, 10, 9].
We take a different viewpoint toward handling stochastic instability.
Recent work [54] showed that the acceleration mechanism in fixed-point and minimax problems is not unique: besides anchor-based algorithms [29, 22, 56, 27, 49], there exist their H-dual algorithms, which display materially different update rules but share exactly the same deterministic last-iterate rate with the optimal anchor-based acceleration.
Hence, the deterministic worst-case complexity alone does not identify a single preferred accelerated method.
In this paper, we demonstrate that the stochastic extension can break this tie: the Stochastic Dual Optimal Halpern Method (S-Dual-OHM) attains $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon$ with ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ oracle complexity, with iteration complexity $N={\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-1})$ and with constant mini-batch size ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ .
This is done cleanly and more directly without assuming diminishing variance or using variance reduction techniques, which is not what stochastic anchoring methods were capable of [27, 10, 9].
In our view, this result has a conceptual implication beyond simply providing another efficient stochastic algorithm.
It challenges the folklore view that acceleration, as studied in deterministic optimization, is brittle and incompatible with stochastic noise.
Equivalently, optimal deterministic methods can behave very differently under stochastic perturbations, and selecting the right representation of acceleration may enable the design of efficient stochastic algorithms.

2 Related Work
Fixed-point problems, Halpern iteration and acceleration.
For fixed-point problems
$\displaystyle\underset{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\text{find}}\quad x={\mathbb{T}}(x),$
(2)
with a nonexpansive operator ${\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , the Halpern iteration $x_{k+1}=\beta_{k}x_{0}+(1-\beta_{k}){\mathbb{T}}x_{k}$ has been studied over a long history in the literature [21, 51], and has been found to provide accelerated worst-case convergence rate $\left\|x_{k}-{\mathbb{T}}(x_{k})\right\|={\mathcal{O}}(1/k)$ with $\beta_{k}={\mathcal{O}}(1/k)$ [45, 16, 29, 22, 26].
The best choice $\beta_{k}=\frac{1}{k+2}$ , due to [29, 22], was later shown to yield an optimal rate $\left\|x_{k}-{\mathbb{T}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{(k+1)^{2}}$ , exactly matching the lower bound [40].
More recently, it was discovered that the same exact optimal rate for a fixed finite horizon $N$ can be achieved by distinct types of acceleration forming an infinite family of algorithms [55] including Dual-OHM [54], the basis of this work.
Acceleration of minimax optimization and monotone inclusion.
Minimax optimization with convex-concave objective can be recast into monotone inclusion, i.e., root-finding problem (1) with saddle gradient operator ${\mathbb{F}}$ , which is a monotone operator [42, 17].
Some early works including [44, 16] first connected Halpern acceleration to these problems, and [16] achieved near-optimal complexity for Lipschitz continuous monotone inclusions.
The remaining logarithmic gap was removed by the Extra Anchored Gradient algorithm [56], and the so-called anchor acceleration for minimax optimization and monotone inclusion has been studied extensively since then [27, 49, 57, 48, 11, 5, 1, 25].
Several works proposed its extension via distinct interpretation in connection with Nesterov momentum [6, 50].
On the other hand, the dual-anchor acceleration analogous to Dual-OHM from fixed-point problems is achievable in this setting as well [54].
Residual-reducing algorithms for stochastic fixed-point and monotone inclusion problems.
The Fast Extragradient algorithm [27], an anchor-based accelerated minimax algorithm, was proposed with its stochastic extension under the assumption that the operator’s variance at $k$ -th iteration diminishes at the order of ${\mathcal{O}}(1/k)$ .
Then [10] showed that stochastic Halpern iteration and its minimax variants, combined with PAGE variance reduction [28] can reduce the complexity of achieving $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon$ to ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ , which could be further improved to $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(n+\sqrt{n}\epsilon^{-1})$ [9] in the finite-sum setting where ${\mathbb{F}}$ is the average of $n=o(\epsilon^{-3})$ sample operators.
Near-optimal complexity of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2})$ for stochastic monotone inclusion has been achieved in [12] with the matching $\Omega(\epsilon^{-2})$ lower bound via more sophisticated recursive anchoring (regularization) algorithm.
On another line of work, [7, 41, 8] studied stochastic Krasnosel’skiĭ–Mann or Halpern iterations in general normed spaces, where the lower bound of $\Omega(\epsilon^{-3})$ was established [8].
Broader view of extending acceleration to stochastic settings.
In smooth convex minimization, it is now standard that accelerated methods are fragile to persistent oracle error: preserving acceleration under stochasticity typically requires either variance reduction or catalyst-type outer–inner regularization [15, 30, 2].
A similar viewpoint has been adopted for monotone root-finding problems in prior works [10, 9, 12].
Our approach departs from this line of work.
Rather than stabilizing Halpern-type acceleration by additional variance-reduction or regularization, we leverage the fact that deterministic acceleration is not unique, and there exist distinct optimal accelerated algorithms with the same worst-case deterministic rate.
We deliver the new observation that among them, Dual-OHM is intrinsically less sensitive to stochastic perturbations than Halpern-type algorithms, enabling direct stochastic extension.
3 Preliminaries and Assumptions
In this section, we establish some necessary preliminary concepts for subsequent analysis.
3.1 Monotonicity, cocoercivity, and nonexpansiveness of operators
We say that an operator ${\mathbb{F}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is
-
Monotone if $\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq 0$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$
-
$\mu$ -strongly monotone if $\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq\mu\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$
-
$M$ -Lipschitz if $\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y)\right\|\leq M\left\|x-y\right\|$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$
-
$\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -Cocoercive if $\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq\frac{1}{L}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y)\right\|^{2}$ for all $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$
where $\mu,M,L>0$ .
In particular, $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercivity implies monotonicity and $L$ -Lipschitzness, but not vice versa.
If ${\mathbb{F}}$ is $M$ -Lipschitz and $\mu$ -strongly monotone, then it is $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercive with $L=\nicefrac{{M^{2}}}{{\mu}}$ [17].
When ${\mathbb{F}}$ is $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercive, the problem (1) can be recast into a fixed-point problem (2) where ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is nonexpansive, i.e., $1$ -Lipschitz for $0<\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L}$ [3, Proposition 4.39], where ${\mathbb{I}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ is the identity operator.
This is because
$\mathrm{Zer}\,({\mathbb{F}}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,{\mathbb{F}}(x)=0\}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,|\,{\mathbb{T}}(x)=x\}:=\mathrm{Fix}\,({\mathbb{T}}).$
Given an unconstrained minimax problem
$\displaystyle\underset{u\in\mathbb{R}^{m}}{\text{minimize}}\,\,\underset{v\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\text{maximize}}\,\,\Phi(u,v)$
(3)
with a convex-concave (resp. $\mu$ -strongly-convex-strongly-concave) and $M$ -smooth $\Phi\colon\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}$ , its saddle operator
${\mathbb{F}}(u,v)=(\nabla_{u}\Phi(u,v),-\nabla_{v}\Phi(u,v))$
is monotone (resp. $\mu$ -strongly monotone) and $M$ -Lipschitz [17].
3.2 Stochastic oracle models
We consider an unbiased stochastic operator oracle with bounded variance.
3.1Stochastic operator oracle.
Given $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , one can query $\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)$ such that
$\mathbb{E}\left[\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)\right]={\mathbb{F}}(x),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)-{\mathbb{F}}(x)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sigma^{2}.$
For our main result (Theorem 4.1), we additionally assume the following condition.
3.2Cocoercivity in expectation.
For any $x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ , we have
$\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L}\left\|\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi)-\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(y;\xi)\right\|^{2}\right].$
This condition holds for some natural cases, including: (i) stochastic oracle with additive noise $\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}(x;\xi)={\mathbb{F}}(x)+\zeta(\xi)$ , where the noise $\zeta(\xi)$ is independent of $x$ , (ii) finite sum or expectation models where each sample operator is $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercive, and as its special case, (iii) sample operators that are each $\mu$ -strongly monotone ( $\mu>0$ ) and $M$ -Lipschitz, which implies sample-wise cocoercivity [17].
Note that 3.2 does not capture general monotone and Lipschitz sample operators or the cases where the noise $\zeta(\xi)$ depends on $x$ .
Similar assumptions or even stronger samplewise cocoercivity have been commonly used in stochastic optimization [39, 2, 32, 37, 4, 58, 52, 14, 9].
Given batch size $B\geq 1$ , we denote ${\mathbb{F}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(x;\xi_{b})$ , where $\xi_{b}$ are conditionally independent samples given $x$ and ${\mathcal{B}}=\{\xi_{1},\dots,\xi_{B}\}$ .
Then ${\mathbb{F}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x)$ is an unbiased estimator of ${\mathbb{F}}(x)$ with $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(x)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}$ and $\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y),x-y\right\rangle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{L}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}_{\mathcal{B}}(y)\right\|^{2}\right]$ by Jensen’s inequality.
For $0<\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L}$ , if we let $\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\cdot;\xi)={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha\widehat{\mathbb{F}}(\cdot;\xi)$ and ${\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{b=1}^{B}\widehat{\mathbb{T}}(\cdot;\xi_{b})$ , then these are unbiased estimators of the nonexpansive ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ with variance bounds $\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}$ and $\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}$ , respectively.
Furthermore, we have: $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x)-{\mathbb{T}}_{\mathcal{B}}(y)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}$ (square-nonexpansivity in expectation).
3.3 Known acceleration in fixed-point problems and its extension
Our general strategy is to reformulate (1) with cocoercive ${\mathbb{F}}$ into (2) with ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ and build upon deterministic acceleration results.
Consider the following algorithms: given $y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ ,
$\displaystyle y_{k+1}=\frac{1}{k+2}y_{0}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}{\mathbb{T}}y_{k}$
(OHM)
and, with the convention ${\mathbb{T}}y_{-1}=y_{0}$ ,
$\displaystyle y_{k+1}=y_{k}+\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\left({\mathbb{T}}y_{k}-{\mathbb{T}}y_{k-1}\right)$
(Dual-OHM)
Here, OHM is an anytime algorithm whose update is defined for all $k=0,1,\dots$ , while for Dual-OHM, the terminal iteration number $N$ is fixed, and the update is defined only for $k=0,\dots,N-2$ .
This is a substantive distinction that makes the two algorithms behave differently under stochastic extension (see the discussion in Section 4.1.1 for further details).
3.3Deterministic acceleration [29, 22, 54].
For a nonexpansive operator ${\mathbb{T}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}$ with a fixed point $y_{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}\,{\mathbb{T}}$ and $N\geq 2$ , both OHM and Dual-OHM exhibit the rate
$\left\|y_{N-1}-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\left\|y_{0}-y_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{N^{2}}.$
With ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ and $\alpha=\frac{1}{L}$ , Proposition 3.3 shows that $\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(y_{N-1})\right\|=\frac{\left\|y_{N-1}-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{N-1})\right\|}{\alpha}\leq\epsilon$ is achieved in ${\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{LD}{\epsilon}\right)$ iterations, where $D=\left\|y_{0}-y_{\star}\right\|$ .
3.4Cai et al. [10], Informal.
Stochastic OHM (S-OHM), which has the update rule $x_{k+1}=\frac{1}{k+2}x_{0}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})$ , maintains the deterministic iteration bound $N={\mathcal{O}}(L\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|/\epsilon)$ if $\mathbb{E}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\lesssim\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{k}$ .
Simple mini-batching with $|{\mathcal{B}}_{k}|=\Theta(k\epsilon^{-2})$ yields ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-4})$ stochastic oracle complexity, and using PAGE variance reduction, this can be reduced to ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-3})$ .
4 Stochastic Dual-Anchor Acceleration
In this section, we analyze Stochastic Dual-OHM (S-Dual-OHM): with ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{-1}}(x_{-1})=x_{0}$ ,
$\displaystyle x_{k+1}=x_{k}+\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})\right)$
(S-Dual-OHM)
for $k=0,\dots,N-2$ , and present the following main result of this paper.
4.1.
Consider the root-finding problem (1) with a solution $x_{\star}$ , satisfying Assumptions 3.1 and 3.2.
Then for $0<\alpha\leq\frac{2}{L}$ , S-Dual-OHM with ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}$ with $|{\mathcal{B}}_{k}|\equiv B$ satisfies
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|\right]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{4\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{\alpha^{2}N^{2}}+\frac{6\sigma^{2}}{B}.$
This shows that we will have $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon$ with $N=\Theta(1/\epsilon)$ and $B=\Theta(1/\epsilon^{2})$ , so in total, ${\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{3})$ oracle complexity.
This also proves an equivalent bound on the expected fixed-point residual $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{N-1}-{\mathbb{T}}(x_{N-1})\right\|\right]^{2}\leq\frac{4\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{N^{2}}+\frac{6\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}$ .
While we defer technical details to Appendix A, we provide the outline of key arguments below.
Then, we show that with the additional assumption of strong monotonicity of ${\mathbb{F}}$ , the total complexity can be reduced to $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}}(1/\epsilon^{2})$ with early stopping.
4.1 Proof outline for Theorem 4.1
We start with showing the identity characterizing the S-Dual-OHM algorithm.
4.2.
For S-Dual-OHM with ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}$ , the following identity holds:
$\displaystyle 0$
$\displaystyle=\frac{(N-1)\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{N,j}{\mathcal{Q}}_{N,j}$
where for $j=1,\dots,N-1$ , $\lambda_{N,j}=\frac{N\alpha}{2(N-j)(N-j+1)}$ and

$\displaystyle 0$
$\displaystyle\geq\frac{(N-1)\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle$
(4)
$\displaystyle\geq\frac{N\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}-\frac{N\alpha^{2}}{8}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2N}\left\|x_{\star}-x_{0}\right\|^{2}$

$\displaystyle 0$
$\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{N\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),x_{\star}-x_{0}\right\rangle\right]-\frac{3(N-1)\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{4B}$
$\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{N\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}-\frac{N\alpha^{2}}{8}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}-\frac{1}{2N}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}\right]-\frac{3(N-1)\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{4B}.$
Rearranging, we have the desired bound of Theorem 4.1.
4.1.1 Intuition for why S-Dual-OHM is stable under noise
The previous analysis shows that the net accumulation of error in S-Dual-OHM is proportional to $\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{N,j}=\frac{\alpha(N-1)}{2}={\mathcal{O}}(N)$ .
On the other hand, stochastic OHM satisfies the similar identity
$\displaystyle 0$
$\displaystyle=\frac{(N-1)\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\nu_{j+1,j}{\mathcal{Q}}_{j+1,j}$
where ${\mathcal{Q}}_{j+1,j}=\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{j},{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j}}(x_{j})\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j}}(x_{j})\right\|^{2}$ but with $\nu_{j+1,j}=\frac{\alpha j(j+1)}{2N}$ .
Here each ${\mathcal{Q}}_{j+1,j}$ is not nonnegative, but contributes a negative lower bound proportional to $\sigma^{2}$ in expectation, which then gets multiplied with $\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\nu_{j+1,j}=\Theta(N^{2})$ and the resulting error term is no longer controllable as in Theorem 4.1.
This is a central difference that makes the dual-anchor mechanism more amenable to stochastic extension than anchoring.
A concurrent work [53] has also provided a related theory that the sum of weights associated with inequalities used in the convergence proof measures a fixed-point algorithm’s robustness to oracle noise, albeit in deterministic setting.
A complementary interpretation is that OHM is an anytime optimal algorithm, achieving the rate $\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k-1})\right\|^{2}\leq\frac{4\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{\alpha^{2}k^{2}}$ for all $k=1,2,\dots$ .
This means that OHM must maintain the optimal guarantee at every iteration, forcing it to exploit the geometric property of ${\mathbb{F}}$ , i.e. cocoercivity, throughout the trajectory.
Its analysis is therefore more tightly tuned to the exact problem class and is more easily disrupted by oracle noise.
Dual-OHM, by contrast, is optimized only for the prescribed last iterate and does not suffer from the same accumulation of errors.
4.2 Faster convergence under strong monotonicity
In this section, we show that if ${\mathbb{F}}$ is additionally strongly monotone, then S-Dual-OHM can achieve the desired accuracy $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|\right]\leq\epsilon$ much earlier than the prescribed horizon $N$ .
Suppose that ${\mathbb{F}}$ is $\mu$ -strongly monotone and $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercive.
Then, with $0<\alpha<\frac{2}{L}$ , ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ is $\gamma$ -contractive (i.e., $\gamma$ -Lipschitz) with $\gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha\mu(2-\alpha L)}$ because
$\displaystyle\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(x)-{\mathbb{T}}(y)\right\|^{2}$
$\displaystyle=\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}-2\alpha\left\langle x-y,{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y)\right\rangle+\alpha^{2}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y)\right\|^{2}$
$\displaystyle\leq\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}-\alpha(2-\alpha L)\left\langle x-y,{\mathbb{F}}(x)-{\mathbb{F}}(y)\right\rangle\leq\left(1-\alpha\mu(2-\alpha L)\right)\left\|x-y\right\|^{2}.$
4.5.
Consider the root-finding problem (1) with $\nicefrac{{1}}{{L}}$ -cocoercive and $\mu$ -strongly monotone ${\mathbb{F}}$ and a solution $x_{\star}$ , satisfying Assumption 3.1.
Let $0<\alpha<\frac{2}{L}$ and $\gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha\mu(2-\alpha L)}\in[0,1)$ .
Let $y_{k}$ be the iterates from deterministic Dual-OHM using true evaluations of ${\mathbb{T}}$ , with $x_{0}=y_{0}$ .
Then, for $k=0,\dots,N-1$ , S-Dual-OHM with ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}$ satisfies
$\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq 2\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{2}\gamma^{2k}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B(1-\gamma^{2})}.$
(7)
In particular, when $\alpha=\frac{1}{L}$ , we have
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq\frac{8L^{2}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{N^{2}}+\frac{64L^{4}}{\mu^{2}}e^{-k\frac{\mu}{L}}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}+\frac{L}{\mu}\frac{4\sigma^{2}}{B}.$
(8)
Notably, Theorem 4.5 uses only 3.1 and cocoercivity of ${\mathbb{F}}$ , but not 3.2.
The reason why this result holds is two-fold.
First, when ${\mathbb{T}}$ is contractive, S-Dual-OHM does not deviate far from deterministic Dual-OHM (Lemma 4.6).
Second, deterministic Dual-OHM achieves the designated terminal accuracy quickly as it linearly converges to its final iterate $y_{N-1}$ (Lemma 4.7).
Because Dual-OHM has a small residual norm guarantee at $y_{N-1}$ , this implies that $x_{k}$ also attains the comparably small residual quickly.
4.6.
Under the conditions of Theorem 4.5,
$\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B(1-\gamma^{2})}$ for $k=0,\dots,N-1$ .
4.7.
Under the conditions of Lemma 4.6, we have $\left\|y_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|\leq\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)\gamma^{k}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|$ for $k=0,\dots,N-1$ and $x_{\star}\in\mathrm{Fix}\,{\mathbb{T}}$ .
Proof of Theorem 4.5.
Note that $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|y_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|^{2}\right]$ .
Then, by Lemmas 4.6 and 4.7, $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{2\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B(1-\gamma^{2})}+2\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{2}\gamma^{2k}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}$ , which proves (7).
With $\alpha=\frac{1}{L}$ , we have $1-\gamma^{2}=\frac{\mu}{L}$ , so combining (7) with Proposition 3.3 we obtain
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(y_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})-{\mathbb{F}}(y_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(y_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]+2L^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{N-1}\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq\frac{8L^{2}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}}{N^{2}}+2L^{2}\left[\frac{2\sigma^{2}}{\mu LB}+2\left(\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\right)^{2}\gamma^{2k}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|^{2}\right].$
where the second inequality uses $L$ -Lipschitzness of ${\mathbb{F}}$ .
Rearranging and using $\gamma<1$ , $(1-\gamma)^{2}\geq\frac{(1-\gamma)^{2}(1+\gamma)^{2}}{4}=\frac{\mu^{2}}{4L^{2}}$ and $\gamma^{2k}=\left(1-\frac{\mu}{L}\right)^{k}\leq e^{-k\frac{\mu}{L}}$ , we obtain (8).
∎
Let $\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|=D$ . The bound (8) shows that if one chooses, e.g., $N\geq\frac{4LD}{\epsilon},k\geq\frac{L}{\mu}\log\frac{256L^{4}D^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon^{2}}$ and $B\geq\frac{16L\sigma^{2}}{\mu\epsilon^{2}}$ , then $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|\right]^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{k})\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\epsilon^{2}$ .
If we run S-Dual-OHM with these choices of $N$ and $B$ , we will achieve the accuracy $\epsilon$ in ${\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{L}{\mu}\log\epsilon^{-1}\right)$ iterations without the need to run all $N$ iterations.
With this early stopping, S-Dual-OHM has improved ${\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{L^{2}\sigma^{2}}{\mu^{2}\epsilon^{2}}\log\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)$ oracle complexity for strongly monotone ${\mathbb{F}}$ ,
which has near-optimal dependence on $\epsilon$ [12].
The dependence on condition number, however, is still suboptimal, and improving it based on the Dual-OHM mechanism remains an open question.
5 Numerical Experiments
We provide numerical evaluations to demonstrate that the dual-anchor mechanism is indeed preferable to anchoring (Halpern algorithms) under stochasticity, and compare the empirical performance of S-Dual-OHM with prior algorithms for stochastic monotone inclusions/fixed-point problems.
We consider three distinct problems: the worst-case nonexpansive fixed-point operator designed by [40], a finite-sum cocoercive operator using uniform random vectors from the unit sphere, and a strongly-convex–strongly-concave minimax problem with Huber-type regularizers following [56, 12].
All experiments were run on a MacBook Air with Apple M3 Chip and 24GB Memory.
Baseline algorithms.
We consider the simple algorithm $x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})$ , which we call Stochastic Gradient Descent-Ascent (SGDA) following the nomenclature of the minimax optimization literature.
We consider the Stochastic Extragradient (SEG) with the update rule
$x_{k+1/2}=x_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k}),\quad x_{k+1}=x_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k+1/2}}(x_{k+1/2}).$
Next, we consider the naive stochastic extension of OHM: $x_{k+1}=\frac{1}{k+2}x_{0}+\frac{k+1}{k+2}\left(x_{k}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})\right)$ , using the same constant mini-batch size as S-Dual-OHM. We refer to this algorithm as S-OHM (with constant $B$ ). This is included to isolate the effect of using the dual-anchor update rather than the usual anchor acceleration and directly contrast the two mechanisms.
We also include the algorithms carefully designed for reducing the expected last-iterate residual.
First, the Halpern-PAGE algorithm from [10] uses the S-OHM update but replaces ${\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})$ by a PAGE-type estimator which uses a larger batch size $s_{1}$ with probability $p_{k}=\min\left\{1,\frac{2}{k+2}\right\}$ and otherwise uses a sample operator difference with batch size $s_{2}$ .
Second, we use the single-loop simplification of RAIN algorithm from [12] used in their experiments, which can be written as
$z_{k+1/2}=z_{k}-\eta\bigl(\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}(z_{k};\xi_{k})+r_{k}(z_{k})\bigr),\qquad z_{k+1}=z_{k}-\eta\bigl(\widehat{{\mathbb{F}}}(z_{k+1/2};\xi_{k+1/2})+r_{k}(z_{k+1/2})\bigr)$
where $r_{k}(x)=\lambda\gamma(S_{k}x-m_{k}),S_{k}=\sum_{t0$ are hyperparameters.
Finally, Halpern-VR-Finite from [9] is similar to Halpern-PAGE but applicable only to finite sum problems of the form ${\mathbb{F}}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\mathbb{F}}(\cdot;\xi_{i})$ and uses either full-batch (true) operator ${\mathbb{F}}$ or batches of size $\lceil\sqrt{n}\rceil$ .
We tune all baseline algorithms and S-Dual-OHM under the same total sample budget $Q=NB$ via grid search, where computing ${\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}$ with $|{\mathcal{B}}_{k}|=B$ counts as $B$ operator evaluations.
The only hyperparameter $B$ for S-Dual-OHM is tuned over $B\in\{1,10,20,50,100\}$ , and S-OHM (Constant $B$ ) is then run with the same selected $B$ .11

6 Conclusion and Open Questions
We demonstrate that dual-anchor acceleration from deterministic root-finding or fixed-point problems admits a direct stochastic extension, unlike anchor-type accelerated algorithms.
This is based on the observation that distinct deterministic accelerations are intrinsically different in their behavior under stochasticity.
We believe that our work enables interesting future work on acceleration and optimal complexities for stochastic optimization.
Two immediate limitations of our work, which thus remain as open questions, are: (1) whether the dual-anchor mechanism extends to root-finding problems with monotone and Lipschitz operators ${\mathbb{F}}$ and (2) whether we can further improve the complexity to the optimal level [12] ${\mathcal{O}}(\epsilon^{-2}\log\epsilon^{-1})$ via dual-anchor acceleration.
As the dual-anchor acceleration also exists in the monotone Lipschitz inclusion setting [54], we believe that the extension would be possible with novel insights not relying on nonexpansivity of ${\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ .
We also expect our result to serve as a baseline that can be combined with broader techniques in stochastic optimization for potentially achieving the optimal complexity.

We denote by ${\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}_{2}\subseteq\dots$ the natural filtration generated by each iteration of the algorithm (selection of batches ${\mathcal{B}}_{i}$ ), i.e., ${\mathcal{F}}_{k}=\sigma\left({\mathcal{B}}_{0},{\mathcal{B}}_{1},\dots,{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}\right)$ .
A.1 Proof of Lemma 4.2
Proof.
The proof is purely algebraic, and does not use any property of ${\mathbb{F}}$ as an operator or stochastic property of the mini-batches ${\mathcal{B}}_{j}$ .
The case $N=1$ is immediate (we have the vacuous identity $0=0$ ), so assume $N\geq 2$ .
For $j=1,\dots,N$ , define
$G_{j}={\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1}),\qquad g_{j}=\frac{\alpha}{2}G_{j},\qquad u_{j}=x_{j-1}-g_{j}.$
Here, ${\mathcal{B}}_{N-1}$ is a ghost (auxiliary) batch introduced only for the analysis, and is not used by the algorithm update; in fact, one may ignore this ghost batch (i.e., safely assume that ${\mathcal{B}}_{N-1}$ is the full batch) and simply let $G_{N}={\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})$ .
With this notation, we have
${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})=x_{j-1}-\alpha G_{j}=x_{j-1}-2g_{j}.$
Because ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{-1}}(x_{-1})=x_{0}$ , the $k=0$ update of S-Dual-OHM is
$x_{1}=x_{0}+\frac{N-1}{N}\bigl({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0}}(x_{0})-x_{0}\bigr).$
Let
$a_{k}=\frac{N-k-1}{N-k},\qquad z_{0}=0,\qquad z_{k+1}={\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-x_{k+1}$
for $k=0,\dots,N-2$ . Then, from the update rule,
$\displaystyle z_{k+1}$
$\displaystyle={\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-x_{k}-a_{k}\bigl({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})\bigr)$
$\displaystyle=(1-a_{k})(x_{k}-2g_{k+1})-x_{k}+a_{k}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})$
$\displaystyle=a_{k}\left({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})-x_{k}\right)-2(1-a_{k})g_{k+1}$
$\displaystyle=\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}z_{k}-\frac{2}{N-k}g_{k+1}.$
(9)
Then, by definition of $z_{k+1}$ ,
$\displaystyle x_{k+1}={\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-z_{k+1}=x_{k}-2g_{k+1}-z_{k+1}.$
(10)
Now define, for $k=0,\dots,N-1$ ,
$V_{k}=-\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\left\|z_{k}+2g_{N}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2}{N-k}\left\langle z_{k}+2g_{N},x_{k}-x_{N-1}\right\rangle.$
Note that $V_{N-1}=0$ .
Next, we analyze $V_{k}-V_{k+1}$ . To simplify the notations, fix $k\in\{0,\dots,N-2\}$ , and write
$m=N-k,\qquad w_{k}=z_{k}+2g_{N},\qquad d_{k}=g_{k+1}-g_{N},\qquad p_{k}=x_{k}-x_{N-1}.$
From (9), we have
$w_{k+1}-2g_{N}=\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}(w_{k}-2g_{N})-\frac{2}{N-k}g_{k+1}\implies w_{k+1}=\frac{m-1}{m}w_{k}-\frac{2}{m}d_{k}$
and (10) yields
$p_{k+1}=x_{k+1}-x_{N-1}=(x_{k}-x_{N-1})-(z_{k+1}+2g_{N})-2(g_{k+1}-g_{N})=p_{k}-w_{k+1}-2d_{k}.$
Plugging the last identity into the expansion of $V_{k}-V_{k+1}$ below, we proceed as
$\displaystyle V_{k}-V_{k+1}$
$\displaystyle=-\frac{m-1}{m}\left\|w_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2}{m}\left\langle w_{k},p_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{m-2}{m-1}\left\|w_{k+1}\right\|^{2}-\frac{2}{m-1}\left\langle w_{k+1},p_{k+1}\right\rangle$
$\displaystyle=-\frac{m-1}{m}\left\|w_{k}\right\|^{2}+\frac{m}{m-1}\left\|w_{k+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\frac{2}{m}w_{k}-\frac{2}{m-1}w_{k+1},p_{k}\right\rangle+\frac{4}{m-1}\left\langle w_{k+1},d_{k}\right\rangle$
$\displaystyle=\frac{4}{m(m-1)}\left\langle p_{k},d_{k}\right\rangle-\frac{4}{m(m-1)}\left\|d_{k}\right\|^{2}$
$\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(N-k)(N-k-1)}\left\langle u_{k+1}-u_{N},g_{k+1}-g_{N}\right\rangle,$
where in the third equality we substituted
$w_{k+1}=\frac{m-1}{m}w_{k}-\frac{2}{m}d_{k}$ and canceled out the terms, and in the last equality we used
$p_{k}-d_{k}=x_{k}-x_{N-1}-(g_{k+1}-g_{N})=u_{k+1}-u_{N}.$
Summing over $k=0,\dots,N-2$ and using $V_{N-1}=0$ yields
$V_{0}=\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{4}{(N-j+1)(N-j)}\left\langle u_{j}-u_{N},g_{j}-g_{N}\right\rangle.$
On the other hand, since $z_{0}=0$ ,
$V_{0}=-\frac{N-1}{N}\left\|2g_{N}\right\|^{2}+\frac{2}{N}\left\langle 2g_{N},x_{0}-x_{N-1}\right\rangle=-\frac{4}{N}\left((N-1)\left\|g_{N}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle g_{N},x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle\right).$
Therefore, equating the two expressions for $V_{0}$ and multiplying $\frac{N}{4}$ throughout, we obtain
$\displaystyle 0=(N-1)\left\|g_{N}\right\|^{2}+\left\langle g_{N},x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\frac{N}{(N-j)(N-j+1)}\left\langle u_{j}-u_{N},g_{j}-g_{N}\right\rangle.$
(11)
Finally, substituting $u_{j}=x_{j-1}-g_{j}$ and $g_{j}=\frac{\alpha}{2}G_{j}$ , we have
$\displaystyle\left\langle u_{j}-u_{N},g_{j}-g_{N}\right\rangle$
$\displaystyle=\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},g_{j}-g_{N}\right\rangle-\left\|g_{j}-g_{N}\right\|^{2}$
$\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left(\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},G_{j}-G_{N}\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|G_{j}-G_{N}\right\|^{2}\right),$
and
$(N-1)\left\|g_{N}\right\|^{2}=\frac{(N-1)\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|G_{N}\right\|^{2},\qquad\left\langle g_{N},x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle=\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle G_{N},x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle.$
Hence, recalling $G_{j}={\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})$ and $\lambda_{N,j}=\frac{N\alpha}{2(N-j)(N-j+1)}$ , (11) is exactly the claimed identity.
∎
A.2 Proof of Lemma 4.3
We can rewrite Lemma 4.2 as
$\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{(N-1)\alpha^{2}}{4}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),x_{N-1}-x_{0}\right\rangle\right]\\
&\quad+\sum_{j=1}^{N-1}\lambda_{N,j}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right].\end{aligned}$
(12)
Now let $e_{j-1}={\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{j-1})$ be the error term at iteration $j-1$ .
Then we can write
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},{\mathbb{F}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|{\mathbb{F}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1})\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\quad+\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle x_{j-1}-x_{N-1},e_{j-1}\right\rangle-\alpha\left\langle{\mathbb{F}}(x_{j-1})-{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),e_{j-1}\right\rangle-\frac{\alpha}{2}\left\|e_{j-1}\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\geq\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle-x_{N-1}+\alpha{\mathbb{F}}(x_{N-1}),e_{j-1}\right\rangle\right]-\frac{\alpha\sigma^{2}}{2B}$
where the last inequality uses cocoercivity of ${\mathbb{F}}$ , $\mathbb{E}\left[e_{j-1}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{j-1}\right]=0$ and $x_{j-1}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}(x_{j-1})$ is deterministic on ${\mathcal{F}}_{j-1}$ , and $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|e_{j-1}\right\|^{2}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{j-1}\right]\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}$ .
Substituting the above into (12) and using ${\mathbb{T}}={\mathbb{I}}-\alpha{\mathbb{F}}$ , we obtain (6).
A.3 Proof of Lemma 4.4
A.1.
For $k=1,\dots,N-1$ , the S-Dual-OHM iterates can be expressed as
$x_{k}=\frac{1}{N}x_{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-2}\frac{1}{(N-t-1)(N-t)}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})+\frac{N-k}{N-k+1}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1}).$
(13)
Proof.
We use induction on $k$ . For $k=1$ , $x_{1}=x_{0}+\frac{N-1}{N}\bigl({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0}}(x_{0})-x_{0}\bigr)=\frac{1}{N}x_{0}+\frac{N-1}{N}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{0}}(x_{0})$ , which agrees with (13).
Now assume (13) holds for some $k\in\{1,\dots,N-2\}$ .
Then, by induction hypothesis,
$\displaystyle x_{k+1}$
$\displaystyle=x_{k}+\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\Bigl({\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})\Bigr)$
$\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}x_{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-2}\frac{1}{(N-t-1)(N-t)}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})+\left(\frac{N-k}{N-k+1}-\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\right){\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k-1}}(x_{k-1})$
$\displaystyle\quad+\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{k}}(x_{k}).$
The induction (hence the proof) is then complete, as $\frac{N-k}{N-k+1}-\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}=\frac{1}{(N-k)(N-k+1)}$ .
∎
A.2Leave-one-out stability.
For $s\in\{0,\dots,N-2\}$ , let $\big\{x_{k}^{(s)}\big\}_{k=0}^{N-1}$ be the trajectory obtained from S-Dual-OHM by replacing only the minibatch ${\mathcal{B}}_{s}$ by an independent copy ${\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{\prime}$ , while keeping all other minibatches the same in the two runs.
Then, for every $k=s+1,\dots,N-1$ ,
$\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-x_{k}^{(s)}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}\left(1+\frac{(N-k)(N-k-1)}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\right).$
(14)
In particular,
$\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{N-1}-x_{N-1}^{(s)}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}.$
(15)
Proof.
Define $w_{k,t}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{(N-t-1)(N-t)},&0\leq t\leq k-2,\\[5.69054pt]
\dfrac{N-k}{N-k+1},&t=k-1\end{cases}$ so that by Lemma A.1,
$x_{k}=\frac{1}{N}x_{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t}),\qquad x_{k}^{(s)}=\frac{1}{N}x_{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}}(x_{t}^{(s)}),$
where ${\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}={\mathcal{B}}_{t}$ for $t\neq s$ , and ${\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{(s)}={\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{\prime}$ .
Since $\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}=1-\frac{1}{N}$ , by Jensen’s inequality,
$\displaystyle\begin{aligned} d_{k}^{(s)}&:=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-x_{k}^{(s)}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}}\big(x_{t}^{(s)}\big)\right\|^{2}\right]\\
&=\sum_{t=s}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}}\big(x_{t}^{(s)}\big)\right\|^{2}\right].\end{aligned}$
(16)
Let $\nu^{2}=\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}$ for simplicity. We will show by induction on $k$ that
$\displaystyle d_{k}^{(s)}\leq\nu^{2}\left(1+\frac{(N-k)(N-k-1)}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\right).$
(17)
For $k=s+1$ , from (16) we have
$\displaystyle d_{s+1}^{(s)}\leq w_{s+1,s}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{s}}(x_{s})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{\prime}}\big(x_{s}\big)\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{N-s-1}{N-s}\cdot 2\nu^{2}$
which agrees with (17). Here we used the fact that ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{s}}(x_{s})$ and ${\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{\prime}}(x_{s})$ are estimators of ${\mathbb{T}}(x_{s})$ of variance $\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}=\nu^{2}$ , and they are conditionally independent on ${\mathcal{F}}_{s}$ .
Next, let $s+2\leq\ell\leq N-1$ , and suppose (17) holds for all $t=s+1,\dots,\ell-1$ .
Note that in (16), we have ${\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}={\mathcal{B}}_{t}$ for $t>s$ , so by 3.2,
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}^{(s)}}\big(x_{t}^{(s)}\big)\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}\big(x_{t}^{(s)}\big)\right\|^{2}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{G}}_{t}^{(s)}\right]\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{t}-x_{t}^{(s)}\right\|^{2}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{G}}_{t}^{(s)}\right]\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{t}-x_{t}^{(s)}\right\|^{2}\right]=d_{t}^{(s)}$
where ${\mathcal{G}}_{t}^{(s)}=\sigma({\mathcal{B}}_{0},\dots,{\mathcal{B}}_{t-1},{\mathcal{B}}_{s}^{\prime})$ is the $\sigma$ -algebra enlarged from ${\mathcal{F}}_{t}$ to make $x_{t}^{(s)}$ measurable.
Applying this to (16) with the induction hypothesis, we obtain
$\displaystyle d_{\ell}^{(s)}$
$\displaystyle\leq 2w_{\ell,s}\nu^{2}+\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}d_{t}^{(s)}$
(18)
$\displaystyle\leq 2w_{\ell,s}\nu^{2}+\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}\nu^{2}\left(1+\frac{(N-t)(N-t-1)}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\right)$
$\displaystyle=\nu^{2}\left[2w_{\ell,s}+\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}\right]+\frac{\nu^{2}}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}(N-t)(N-t-1).$
Now using the explicit formula for $w_{\ell,t}$ , we can directly verify
$2w_{\ell,s}+\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}=1-\frac{1}{N-s}+\frac{1}{(N-s)(N-s-1)},$
and
$\displaystyle\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-1}w_{\ell,t}(N-t)(N-t-1)=\sum_{t=s+1}^{\ell-2}1+\frac{N-\ell}{N-\ell+1}(N-\ell+1)(N-\ell)$
$\displaystyle=\ell-s-2+(N-\ell)^{2}=N-s-2+(N-\ell)(N-\ell-1).$
Hence
$\displaystyle d_{\ell}^{(s)}$
$\displaystyle\leq\nu^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{N-s}\right)+\frac{\nu^{2}}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\Bigl(N-s-1+(N-\ell)(N-\ell-1)\Bigr)$
$\displaystyle=\nu^{2}\left(1+\frac{(N-\ell)(N-\ell-1)}{(N-s)(N-s-1)}\right),$
which completes the induction, proving (14).
Then (15) follows immediately by setting $k=N-1$ .
∎
Now we are ready to prove Lemma 4.4.
Fix $j\in\{1,\dots,N-1\}$ , and let $x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}$ denote the leave-one-out terminal iterate from Lemma A.2, obtained by replacing only the minibatch ${\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}$ by an independent copy.
By the fact that $x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}$ is conditionally independent with $e_{j-1}$ (which depends only on ${\mathcal{B}}_{j-1}$ ) on ${\mathcal{F}}_{j-1}$ and the tower property, we have $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle{\mathbb{T}}\big(x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}\big),e_{j-1}\right\rangle\right]=0$ .
Hence
$\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle e_{j-1},{\mathbb{T}}x_{N-1}\right\rangle\right]$
$\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle e_{j-1},{\mathbb{T}}(x_{N-1})-{\mathbb{T}}\big(x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}\big)\right\rangle\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|e_{j-1}\right\|^{2}\right]\,\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(x_{N-1})-{\mathbb{T}}\big(x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}\big)\right\|^{2}\right]}$
$\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{N-1}-x_{N-1}^{(j-1)}\right\|^{2}\right]}$
$\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\frac{\sigma^{2}}{B}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}}=\frac{\alpha\sigma^{2}}{B}.$
A.4 Proof of Lemma 4.6
Let
$s_{k}=\mathbb E\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|^{2},\qquad M_{k}=\max_{0\leq i\leq k}s_{i}.$
It suffices to show that $M_{k}\leq\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B(1-\gamma^{2})}$ .
For $k=0$ , we have $x_{0}=y_{0}$ , so we have $s_{0}=M_{0}=0$ and the result is trivial.
Let $k\geq 1$ . Applying Lemma A.1 for both S-Dual-OHM and its full-batch version (Dual-OHM), we obtain
$x_{k}-y_{k}=\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\left[{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})+e_{t}\right]$
where $e_{t}={\mathbb{T}}_{{\mathcal{B}}_{t}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})$ and $w_{k,t}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{1}{(N-t-1)(N-t)},&0\leq t\leq k-2\\
\dfrac{N-k}{N-k+1},&t=k-1\end{cases}$ .
Note that here $\mathbb{E}\left[e_{t}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\right]=0$ and $\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|e_{t}\right\|^{2}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\right]\leq\nu^{2}:=\frac{\alpha^{2}\sigma^{2}}{B}$ .
Using $\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}=1-\frac{1}{N}<1$ and Jensen’s inequality gives
$\displaystyle s_{k}=\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{k}-y_{k}\right\|^{2}\right]\leq\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})+e_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]$
$\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\left(\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})\right\|^{2}+\left\|e_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]\right)\leq\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}w_{k,t}\left(\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|x_{t}-y_{t}\right\|^{2}\right]+\nu^{2}\right)\leq\gamma^{2}M_{k-1}+\nu^{2}$
where for the second inequality, we used conditional unbiasedness together with the tower property:
$\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle e_{t},{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})\right\rangle\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left[\left\langle e_{t},{\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})\right\rangle\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\right]\right]=0$
which holds because ${\mathbb{T}}(x_{t})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{t})$ is deterministic conditioned on ${\mathcal{F}}_{t}$ and $\mathbb{E}\left[e_{t}\,\middle|\,{\mathcal{F}}_{t}\right]=0$ .
Now starting with $M_{0}=0$ and using induction on $k$ , the above shows that
$M_{k}\leq\max\left\{M_{k-1},\gamma^{2}M_{k-1}+\nu^{2}\right\}\leq\max\left\{M_{k-1},\gamma^{2}\frac{\nu^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}}+\nu^{2}\right\}=\frac{\nu^{2}}{1-\gamma^{2}}.$
A.5 Proof of Lemma 4.7
Observe that for $k=1,2,\dots$ ,
$\displaystyle\left\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\|=\frac{N-k-1}{N-k}\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(y_{k})-{\mathbb{T}}(y_{k-1})\right\|\leq\gamma\left\|y_{k}-y_{k-1}\right\|$
$\displaystyle\implies\left\|y_{k+1}-y_{k}\right\|\leq\gamma^{k}\left\|y_{1}-y_{0}\right\|=\gamma^{k}\left\|\frac{N-1}{N}({\mathbb{T}}(y_{0})-y_{0})\right\|\leq\gamma^{k}(1+\gamma)\left\|y_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|$
where the last inequality uses $\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(y_{0})-y_{0}\right\|\leq\left\|{\mathbb{T}}(y_{0})-x_{\star}\right\|+\left\|x_{\star}-y_{0}\right\|\leq(1+\gamma)\left\|y_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|$ (recall that we assume $x_{0}=y_{0}$ ).
Thus
$\displaystyle\left\|y_{N-1}-y_{k}\right\|$
$\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j=k}^{N-2}\left\|y_{j+1}-y_{j}\right\|\leq(1+\gamma)\left\|y_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|\sum_{j=k}^{N-2}\gamma^{j}\leq\frac{1+\gamma}{1-\gamma}\gamma^{k}\left\|x_{0}-x_{\star}\right\|.$
Appendix B Additional Experiments
In the experiments of Section 5, we used for S-OHM the same batch size $B$ selected for S-Dual-OHM.
This was designed to isolate the effect of replacing the anchor update by the dual-anchor update while
keeping all the other configurations identical.
However, to verify that the observed empirical advantage of S-Dual-OHM is not an artifact of this experiment design, we repeat all four experiments while separately tuning the batch size of S-OHM over the same grid $B\in\{1,10,20,50,100\}$ , under the same total sample budget $Q=NB$ .
All the other experimental settings are unchanged.
For simplicity, we only compare the results from S-Dual-OHM with tuned $B$ and runs of S-OHM using the shared and tuned batch sizes.
