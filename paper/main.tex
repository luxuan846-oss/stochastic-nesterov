\begin{abstract}
We consider the problem of finding a fixed point of an averaged operator
in a real Hilbert space using stochastic oracle evaluations. The operator and its fixed-point set are deterministic, whereas
the oracle responses and the iterates are random. We focus on a stochastic
implementation of the Fast Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann method that uses one new
oracle evaluation per iteration and retains the preceding evaluation.
The objective is to identify noise conditions under which the last-iterate
fixed-point residual decays at an accelerated almost-sure rate and the
iterates converge weakly almost surely. This initial draft specifies the
problem, the oracle model, and the iteration; stochastic convergence
theorems and sharp noise thresholds remain to be established.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Key words.} averaged operator, fixed-point problem,
Fast KM, stochastic oracle, almost-sure convergence.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

We consider the problem of finding a fixed point of an averaged operator
$T:\HH\to\HH$ in a real Hilbert space when only noisy evaluations of $T$
are available. Many single-loop stochastic first-order methods are
stochastic Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann (KM) iterations of the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-skm}
 x_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_k)x_k+\lambda_k\bigl(Tx_k+e_k\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $e_k$ is the error of the $k$th oracle response
\cite{combettes2015stochastic,bravo2024stochastic,cortild2026stochastic}.
Stochastic gradient descent, the stochastic proximal gradient method and
stochastic backward--forward splitting are instances of this form; in the
proximal gradient method, the error is in general conditionally biased.

For exact evaluations, the fixed-point residual of KM with a constant
relaxation parameter satisfies $\norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(1/\sqrt{k})$
\cite[Section~1]{bot2023fast}. The Fast KM method of Bo\c{t} and Nguyen
\cite[Algorithm~2.1]{bot2023fast} improves this to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-fast-km-rate}
 \norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(1/k),\qquad x_k\rightharpoonup x^\star\in\Fix T,
\end{equation}
with parameters that are fixed and do not vanish
\cite[Theorems~3.4--3.5]{bot2023fast}; see Section~\ref{sec:deterministic-fast-km}. Each update uses $Tx_k$ and the
value $Tx_{k-1}$ computed in the preceding step. The forward--backward
map above is covered by \cite[Corollary~4.1]{bot2023fast}.

With noisy evaluations, a single-query implementation stores the response
$\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})=Tx_{k-1}+e_{k-1}$ and uses it again in the
next update. The error of the update then reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intro-error}
 \eta_k=\frac{s(k+\alpha/2)}{k+\alpha}\,e_k-\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}\,e_{k-1},
 \qquad
 \EE[\eta_k\mid\FF_k]=-\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}\,e_{k-1};
\end{equation}
see Section~\ref{sec:iteration}. Two features distinguish this recursion
from \eqref{eq:intro-skm}. First, the stored error $e_{k-1}$ is known
before the current query, so $\eta_k$ is not a martingale difference.
Second, the coefficient of the new error tends to $s$, so the parameters
do not damp persistent noise. For $T=0$ and $s=1$, the iteration becomes
$x_{k+1}=a_kx_k+a_ke_k-d_ke_{k-1}$ with $a_k=\frac{k+\alpha/2}{k+\alpha}
\geq\frac12$ and $d_k=\frac{k}{k+\alpha}$. If
$\EE[\norm{e_k}^2\mid\FF_k]=\sigma^2>0$, conditional unbiasedness gives
$\EE\norm{x_{k+1}-Tx_{k+1}}^2\geq\sigma^2/4$ for every $k\geq1$. Under
bounded variance (N2), the residual therefore need not vanish. In
contrast, stochastic KM tolerates bounded variance through vanishing
relaxation parameters \cite[Theorem~2.5]{bravo2024stochastic}.

These observations lead to the question studied in this paper:
\begin{quote}
\emph{For the single-query stochastic Fast KM method with fixed
parameters, how fast must the variance $\sigma_k^2$ of the oracle errors
decay for $k\norm{x_k-Tx_k}\to0$ and $x_k\rightharpoonup x_\infty\in\Fix T$
to hold almost surely along the same trajectory? Are the decaying-noise
conditions (N6-$\ell^2$), $\sum_k(k+1)^2\sigma_k^2<\infty$, and
(N6-$\ell^1$), $\sum_k(k+1)\sigma_k<\infty$, required by the algorithm
or by the available proof technique?}
\end{quote}
With mini-batches of size $m_k\asymp k^q$ and $\sigma_k^2=\sigma^2/m_k$,
(N6-$\ell^2$) holds if and only if $q>3$, and (N6-$\ell^1$) if and only
if $q>4$ (Remark~\ref{rem:batches}). The order $k^{3+\omega}$ is the
batch growth used for related accelerated methods
\cite[Section~4.2 and Corollary~2]{nguyentrung2025afp}.

\paragraph{Objectives.}
For the recursion of Section~\ref{sec:iteration}, under (N1) and
finite second moments, we aim to establish:
\begin{enumerate}
 \item almost-sure weak convergence of the iterates and vanishing residual
 under the variance budget $\sum_k\sigma_k^2<\infty$, that is, $q>1$,
 which is weaker than (N6-$\ell^1$);
 \item the almost-sure rate $k\norm{x_k-Tx_k}\to0$ under (N6-$\ell^2$);
 \item explicit examples that decide whether the weight $(k+1)^2$ in
 (N6-$\ell^2$) can be reduced; for $T=0$, the mean-square residual rate
 requires only $q>2$, whereas $T=-\Id$ shows no cancellation of the
 stored error;
 \item applications to stochastic gradient, proximal gradient and
 backward--forward methods, the proximal gradient method under the bias
 condition (N7).
\end{enumerate}
The statements are research objectives of this draft; none of them is
proved here.

\paragraph{Organization.}
Section~\ref{sec:problem} specifies the operator, the oracle model and
the noise conditions. Section~\ref{sec:algorithm} recalls KM and Fast KM
and states the stochastic recursion. Section~\ref{sec:applications}
identifies stochastic gradient, proximal gradient and backward--forward
methods as instances of the model.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related-work}

\paragraph{Stochastic KM iterations.}
Bravo and Cominetti \cite{bravo2024stochastic} study stochastic KM in
finite-dimensional normed spaces. Their Theorem~2.5 gives almost-sure
convergence of the iterates for martingale-difference noise with finite
second moments, under summability conditions that hold for bounded
variance and $\lambda_k=(k+1)^{-a}$ with $2/3<a\leq1$. Their Theorem~2.8
treats decaying, possibly biased errors with
$\sum_k\lambda_k\EE[\norm{e_k}\mid\FF_{k}]<\infty$ almost surely,
allowing constant relaxation parameters; it yields convergence of the
iterates without a rate. Combettes and Pesquet
\cite[Corollary~2.7]{combettes2015stochastic} obtain the corresponding
weak convergence in separable Hilbert spaces. Cortild, Cartis and
Peypouquet \cite{cortild2026stochastic} replace the uniform variance
bound by finite variance at one fixed point and treat stochastic
gradient and stochastic backward--forward splitting. In these results,
the noise condition is balanced by the relaxation sequence; the Fast KM
recursion has no such sequence.

\paragraph{Fast deterministic fixed-point and splitting methods.}
Fast KM \cite{bot2023fast} is extended by Bo\c{t}, Chenchene and Fadili
\cite{bot2026overrelaxation} to overrelaxation and degenerate
preconditioners, which include primal--dual splitting. Bo\c{t}, Nguyen
and Zong \cite{bot2026fastfb,bot2025fastrfb} obtain $o(1/k)$ rates for
forward--backward and reflected forward--backward methods by working
with the operator structure of the inclusion directly; these results are
not consequences of Fast KM applied to an averaged map. The order
$O(1/k)$ is worst-case optimal for nonexpansive operators
\cite[Theorem~4.6]{park2022exact}. Yang et al.\
\cite[Appendix~A]{yang2026ikm} identify Fast KM as a parameter choice of
the inertial KM iteration and note that existing analyses concern exact
evaluations or summable perturbations.

\paragraph{Stochastic accelerated methods.}
Almost-sure $o(1/k)$ residual rates are available for accelerated
stochastic methods under three mechanisms.
\begin{itemize}
 \item \emph{Variance reduction.} VR-FKM \cite{trandinh2024vrfkm}
 (finite sums), VFOSA \cite[Theorem~14]{trandinh2025vfosa} and VAPEG
 \cite[Corollary~3.4]{trandinh2025vapeg}. In VFOSA, the squared estimator error is
 summable with the weight $(k+1)^2$ almost surely, which is the weight
 in (N6-$\ell^2$).
 \item \emph{Growing mini-batches.} The AFP method
 \cite[Corollary~2]{nguyentrung2025afp} uses delayed oracle values and
 batches of order $k^{3+\omega}$, $\omega>0$.
 \item \emph{Finite horizon or anchoring.} Yang et al.\
 \cite[Theorem~1]{yang2026ikm} bound the root-mean-square last-iterate
 residual by $O(D/K+\sigma\log K/\sqrt{K})$ with one response per
 iteration and parameters depending on the horizon $K$. Anchored methods
 \cite{lee2026fixed,alacaoglu2026anytime} give expected last-iterate
 guarantees.
\end{itemize}
None of these results concerns the recursion of
Section~\ref{sec:iteration}, which uses one fresh response per iteration,
reuses the stored response and applies no variance reduction.

\paragraph{Complexity lower bounds.}
For stochastic oracles, $\Omega(\varepsilon^{-2})$ samples are necessary
\cite[Theorem~2]{foster2019complexity}. Lee and Kim
\cite{lee2026fixed} show that the complexity
$O(LD/\sqrt{\varepsilon}+\sigma^2/\varepsilon)$ is unattainable in general
for monotone Lipschitz inclusions under their oracle assumptions. These bounds concern expected
accuracy after a prescribed number of queries. They do not determine the
noise decay required for an almost-sure rate along one trajectory of a
fixed recursion, which is the question above.

\subsection{Notation and preliminaries}\label{sec:notation}

Throughout, $\HH$ is a real separable Hilbert space with inner product
$\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$, associated norm $\norm{\cdot}$ and Borel
sigma-algebra $\mathcal B$. $\Id$ denotes the identity operator on $\HH$,
and $\rightharpoonup$ and $\to$ denote weak and strong convergence in
$\HH$, respectively. An operator $R:\HH\to\HH$ is nonexpansive if
$\norm{Rx-Ry}\leq\norm{x-y}$ for all $x,y\in\HH$. Following
\cite[Section~1]{bot2023fast}, $T:\HH\to\HH$ is \emph{$\theta$-averaged},
$\theta\in(0,1]$, if $T=(1-\theta)\Id+\theta R$ for a nonexpansive $R$;
the nonexpansive operators are exactly the $1$-averaged ones. The set of
fixed points of $T$ is $\Fix T:=\{x\in\HH:Tx=x\}$. With $V:=\Id-T$, the
operator $T$ is $\theta$-averaged if and only if $V$ is
$\frac{1}{2\theta}$-cocoercive \cite[equation~(2.1)]{bot2023fast}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocoercivity}
 \ip{Vx-Vy}{x-y}\geq\frac{1}{2\theta}\norm{Vx-Vy}^{2},\qquad x,y\in\HH.
\end{equation}
For a proper, convex and lower semicontinuous $g:\HH\to(-\infty,+\infty]$ and
$\gamma>0$,
\[
 \prox_{\gamma g}(x):=\argmin_{y\in\HH}\Bigl\{g(y)+\frac{1}{2\gamma}
 \norm{y-x}^2\Bigr\}.
\]
The underlying probability space is $(\Omega,\FF,\PP)$. An $\HH$-valued
random variable is a measurable map $x:(\Omega,\FF)\to(\HH,\mathcal B)$.
Let $\mathscr F=(\FF_k)_{k\geq0}$ be a filtration of sub-sigma-algebras of
$\FF$. Following \cite[Notation~2.1]{combettes2015stochastic}, we denote by
$\ell_+(\mathscr F)$ the set of sequences $(\rho_k)_{k\geq0}$ of
$[0,+\infty)$-valued random variables such that $\rho_k$ is
$\FF_k$-measurable for every $k$, and set
\[
 \ell^p_+(\mathscr F):=\Bigl\{(\rho_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell_+(\mathscr F):
 \sum_{k\geq0}\rho_k^p<+\infty\ \ \PP\text{-a.s.}\Bigr\},
 \qquad p\in(0,+\infty).
\]
Equalities and inequalities between random variables hold $\PP$-almost
surely unless stated otherwise. For positive sequences, $a_k\asymp b_k$
means that $a_k/b_k$ is bounded from above and bounded away from $0$
\cite[Section~1.2]{bravo2024stochastic}; $O(\cdot)$ and $o(\cdot)$ refer
to $k\to\infty$.

\section{Problem formulation and stochastic oracle}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Averaged fixed-point problem}\label{sec:fixed-point}

\begin{assumption}[Operator and solution set]\label{ass:operator}
The deterministic operator $T:\HH\to\HH$ is $\theta$-averaged for some
$\theta\in(0,1]$, and $\Fix T$ is nonempty.
\end{assumption}

The problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:problem}
 \text{find }x^\star\in\HH\quad\text{such that}\quad Tx^\star=x^\star.
\end{equation}
We measure accuracy by the fixed-point residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 r(x):=\norm{x-Tx},\qquad r_k:=r(x_k).
\end{equation}
Problem~\eqref{eq:problem} is equivalently $Vx^\star=0$, and
$V$ satisfies~\eqref{eq:cocoercivity}. The residual does not by itself provide a bound on the distance to
$\Fix T$ without an additional error-bound condition.

\subsection{Stochastic oracle and noise conditions}\label{sec:oracle}

The sigma-algebra $\FF_k$ of the filtration $\mathscr F$ contains all
information available immediately before query $k$. At an
$\FF_k$-measurable point $x_k$, the oracle returns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle}
 \widehat T_k(x_k)=Tx_k+e_k,\qquad e_k=b_k+\xi_k,
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bias-noise}
 b_k:=\EE[e_k\mid\FF_k],\qquad \EE[\xi_k\mid\FF_k]=0.
\end{equation}
This is the oracle model of \cite[Section~2.2, equation~(6)]{yang2026ikm}:
$b_k$ is $\FF_k$-measurable, and $\xi_k$ is $\FF_{k+1}$-measurable and
conditionally centered. Independence of errors across iterations is not
assumed.

\begin{assumption}[Finite second moments]\label{ass:moments}
For every $k\geq0$, $\EE\norm{e_k}^2<\infty$.
\end{assumption}

Assumption~\ref{ass:moments} corresponds to the finite-variance part of
(H1) in \cite[Section~2.2]{bravo2024stochastic}; it does not require the
errors to decay. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sigma}
 \sigma_k^2:=\EE[\norm{\xi_k}^2\mid\FF_k].
\end{equation}
We use the following noise conditions; (N6) is the condition of
\cite[Theorem~3.1]{attouch2026ravine}.
\begin{description}
 \item[\textnormal{(N1)}] Conditional unbiasedness: $b_k=0$ for all $k$.
 \item[\textnormal{(N2)}] Bounded variance: $\sigma_k^2\leq\sigma^2$
 almost surely for all $k$, for some constant $\sigma\geq0$.
 \item[\textnormal{(N6)}] Decaying noise:
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:n6}
  (s_kt_k\sigma_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell^2_+(\mathscr F)
  \qquad\text{or}\qquad
  (s_kt_k\sigma_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell^1_+(\mathscr F).
 \end{equation}
\end{description}
In (N6), $s_k$ is the step size and $t_k:=1+\sum_{i\geq k}\prod_{j=k}^{i}
\alpha_j$ is generated by the extrapolation coefficients $\alpha_k$.
In \cite[Theorem~3.1]{attouch2026ravine}, the $\ell^2$ form, condition
$(K_2)$, makes the noise term of the one-step energy inequality summable
and yields the rate; the $\ell^1$ form, condition $(K_2^+)$, is used for
the convergence of the iterates.

For the Fast KM recursion of Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, we take
$s_k\equiv s$ and $\alpha_k=\frac{k}{k+\alpha}$, the factor shared by the
two momentum terms of~\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km}. Then
$t_k=1+\alpha_kt_{k+1}$ gives $t_k=\frac{k+\alpha-1}{\alpha-1}$, and
the two forms of (N6) read
\begin{equation}\label{eq:n6-fast-km}
 \text{(N6-$\ell^2$)}\quad \sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)^2\sigma_k^2<\infty,
 \qquad
 \text{(N6-$\ell^1$)}\quad \sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)\sigma_k<\infty,
\end{equation}
almost surely. This transfer of (N6) to Fast KM is a convention of the
present paper. We use (N6) as a candidate sufficient condition; no
convergence theorem for the recursion below is established in this draft.

For biased oracles, we add the following weighted summability of the bias,
modeled on condition (H3) of \cite[Theorem~2.8]{bravo2024stochastic}:
\begin{description}
 \item[\textnormal{(N7)}] Summable weighted bias:
 $(s_kt_k\norm{b_k})_{k\geq0}\in\ell^1_+(\mathscr F)$.
\end{description}
The bias is not averaged out by martingale cancellations, so (N7) is an
$\ell^1$ condition with the weights of (N6). For Fast KM, it reads
$\sum_{k\geq0}(k+1)\norm{b_k}<\infty$ almost surely; whether this weight
is needed is part of the question studied here. Condition (N7) arises for
the stochastic proximal gradient method of Section~\ref{sec:app-fb}.

\begin{remark}[Noise conditions and mini-batches]\label{rem:batches}
Stochastic KM tolerates persistent noise through vanishing relaxation
parameters: with $\lambda_k=(k+1)^{-a}$ and $2/3<a\leq1$, bounded
variance suffices for the almost-sure convergence of the iterates
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{bravo2024stochastic}. Condition (H3) of
\cite[Theorem~2.8]{bravo2024stochastic} instead requires
$\sum_k\lambda_k\EE[\norm{e_k}\mid\FF_k]<\infty$; it admits bias and
constant $\lambda_k$ and gives convergence of the iterates without a
rate. The Fast KM recursion has fixed parameters, so the noise itself
must decay. If each response averages $m_k$ conditionally independent
samples of variance $\sigma^2$, then $\sigma_k^2=\sigma^2/m_k$. For
$m_k\asymp k^q$, (N6-$\ell^2$) holds if and only if $q>3$, and
(N6-$\ell^1$) holds if and only if $q>4$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The target is deterministic]\label{rem:target}
The operator $T$ and the set $\Fix T$ are deterministic. The oracle
responses and the iterates are random. A limit selected by the iteration
may consequently be a random variable with values in $\Fix T$.
The model does not require the sampled mappings to have a common fixed
point or to be individually nonexpansive.
\end{remark}

\section{Fast KM iteration}\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{The Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann iteration}\label{sec:km}

For the fixed-point problem~\eqref{eq:problem}, the Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann
(KM) iteration takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:km}
 x_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_k)x_k+\lambda_k Tx_k,\qquad k\geq0,
\end{equation}
where $x_0\in\HH$ and $\lambda_k\in(0,1)$ is a relaxation parameter.
Each update is a convex combination of the current iterate and its image
under $T$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:operator}, for a fixed relaxation
parameter $\lambda_k\equiv\lambda\in(0,1)$, the iterates converge weakly
to a point in $\Fix T$ and the fixed-point residual satisfies
$\norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(k^{-1/2})$; see
\cite[Section~1, equation~(1.3)]{bot2023fast}.

\subsection{The Fast KM method}\label{sec:deterministic-fast-km}

Bo\c{t} and Nguyen \cite{bot2023fast} accelerate the KM iteration using
Nesterov's momentum updates. For fixed parameters $\alpha>2$ and
$0<s\leq1/\theta$, and initial points $x_0,x_1\in\HH$, their Fast KM method is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deterministic-fast-km}
\begin{split}
 x_{k+1}={}&\left(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}Tx_k\\
 &+\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}),\qquad k\geq1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This is \cite[Algorithm~2.1, equation~(2.7)]{bot2023fast}. The update involves both the difference of successive
iterates and the difference of successive operator values.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:operator},
\cite[Theorems~3.4--3.5]{bot2023fast} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deterministic-guarantees}
 \norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(1/k),\qquad
 x_k\rightharpoonup x^\star\in\Fix T.
\end{equation}

For $s=1$, the coefficient of $x_k-x_{k-1}$ in
\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km} vanishes, and the iteration becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-km-unit-step}
 x_{k+1}=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}Tx_k
 +\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}).
\end{equation}
As observed in \cite[Remark~2.1]{bot2023fast}, this is a KM iteration
with the additional extrapolation term
$\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1})$. The extrapolation acts on the
operator values. For $s\neq1$, the explicit inertial term
$\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})$ is also present.

\subsection{Stochastic Fast KM}\label{sec:iteration}

We replace the exact evaluations in~\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km}
by the oracle responses~\eqref{eq:oracle} of Section~\ref{sec:oracle}.
Choose deterministic $x_0,x_1\in\HH$ and fixed $\alpha>2$,
$0<s\leq1/\theta$.
Initialize by computing and storing $\widehat T_0(x_0)$. For every $k\geq1$,
compute $\widehat T_k(x_k)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stochastic-fast-km}
\begin{split}
 x_{k+1}={}&\left(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\widehat T_k(x_k)\\
 &+\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}
 \bigl(\widehat T_k(x_k)-\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The preceding response $\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})$ is stored from the
previous iteration. Hence, after initialization, each update requires one
new oracle evaluation. The points $x_{k-1}$ and $x_k$, together with the
stored response, are $\FF_k$-measurable; $x_{k+1}$ is
$\FF_{k+1}$-measurable. The parameters are independent of a terminal horizon.

Substituting~\eqref{eq:oracle} into the difference of oracle responses yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle-difference}
 \widehat T_k(x_k)-\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})
 =Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}+e_k-e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Relative to the exact update~\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km} evaluated
at the current iterates, the total additive error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbed-recurrence}
 \eta_k=\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}e_k
       +\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}(e_k-e_{k-1})
       =\frac{s(k+\alpha/2)}{k+\alpha}e_k
       -\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Since $e_{k-1}$ is $\FF_k$-measurable, \eqref{eq:bias-noise} gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-update-error}
 \EE[\eta_k\mid\FF_k]=\frac{s(k+\alpha/2)}{k+\alpha}\,b_k
 -\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}\,e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Under (N1), only the stored error remains in the conditional mean.
These identities describe how the current and preceding oracle errors
enter the stochastic iteration.

Our objective is to identify noise conditions under which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-rate}
 k\norm{x_k-Tx_k}\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{almost surely},
\end{equation}
and, for a $\Fix T$-valued random variable $x_\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-iterates}
 x_k\rightharpoonup x_\infty\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}

\section{Applications to stochastic optimization}\label{sec:applications}

This section identifies three single-loop stochastic methods for
composite convex optimization as instances of the oracle model of
Section~\ref{sec:oracle}. For each method we give the operator $T$, its
averagedness constant, its fixed points, the oracle error and the noise
conditions of Section~\ref{sec:oracle} that the sampling scheme
satisfies. The identification of stochastic gradient descent with
stochastic KM follows \cite[Section~4.3]{bravo2024stochastic}; the
splitting operators follow \cite[Section~4]{bot2023fast} and
\cite[Section~3.2]{cortild2026stochastic}.

\subsection{Setting}\label{sec:app-setting}

Consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:composite}
 \min_{x\in\HH}\; h(x)+g(x),\qquad h(x)=\EE_{\zeta}[H(x,\zeta)].
\end{equation}

\begin{assumption}[Composite problem]\label{ass:composite}
The function $h:\HH\to\RR$ is convex and differentiable, and $\nabla h$
is $L$-Lipschitz continuous for some $L>0$. The function
$g:\HH\to(-\infty,+\infty]$ is proper, convex and lower semicontinuous,
and $\argmin(h+g)\neq\emptyset$.
\end{assumption}

By the Baillon--Haddad theorem \cite{baillon1977quelques}, $\nabla h$ is
$\frac1L$-cocoercive. A point $p\in\HH$ minimizes $h+g$ if and only if
$0\in\partial g(p)+\nabla h(p)$.

\begin{assumption}[Sampled gradients]\label{ass:sampled-gradient}
At an $\FF_k$-measurable query point $y_k$, the gradient oracle returns
an $\FF_{k+1}$-measurable vector $u_k$ such that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-noise}
 \EE[u_k\mid\FF_k]=\nabla h(y_k),\qquad
 \EE\bigl[\norm{u_k-\nabla h(y_k)}^2\bigm|\FF_k\bigr]\leq\tau_k^2,
\end{equation}
where $(\tau_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell_+(\mathscr F)$.
\end{assumption}

The unbiasedness in~\eqref{eq:gradient-noise} must be justified by the
sampling model and, when $u_k$ is built from $\nabla_xH$, by the
interchange of differentiation and expectation. If $u_k$ averages
$m_k$ samples $\nabla_xH(y_k,\zeta_{k,j})$, $j=1,\dots,m_k$, that are
conditionally independent given $\FF_k$ and have conditional variance at
most $\tau^2$, then $\tau_k^2\leq\tau^2/m_k$.

The fixed-point residual of each operator below controls a
first-order optimality measure of~\eqref{eq:composite}.

\begin{lemma}[Residuals as optimality measures]\label{lem:residual-optimality}
Let Assumption~\ref{ass:composite} hold, let $\gamma>0$, and let
$x\in\HH$.
\begin{enumerate}
 \item If $p=\prox_{\gamma g}(x-\gamma\nabla h(x))$, then
 $\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial g(p)+\nabla h(p)\bigr)
 \leq(\gamma^{-1}+L)\norm{x-p}$.
 \item If $p=\prox_{\gamma g}(x)$ and $T_{BF}x:=p-\gamma\nabla h(p)$,
 then $\operatorname{dist}\bigl(0,\partial g(p)+\nabla h(p)\bigr)
 \leq\gamma^{-1}\norm{x-T_{BF}x}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) The optimality condition of the proximal step gives
$\gamma^{-1}(x-p)-\nabla h(x)\in\partial g(p)$. Adding $\nabla h(p)$ and
using the Lipschitz continuity of $\nabla h$ yields the bound.
(ii) The optimality condition gives $\gamma^{-1}(x-p)\in\partial g(p)$,
and $\gamma^{-1}(x-p)+\nabla h(p)=\gamma^{-1}(x-T_{BF}x)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Stochastic gradient descent}\label{sec:app-sgd}

Let $g=0$ and $0<\gamma<2/L$. Since $\Id-\frac{2}{L}\nabla h$ is
nonexpansive \cite[Section~4.3]{bravo2024stochastic},
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-map}
 T_G:=\Id-\gamma\nabla h
 =\Bigl(1-\frac{\gamma L}{2}\Bigr)\Id
 +\frac{\gamma L}{2}\Bigl(\Id-\frac{2}{L}\nabla h\Bigr)
\end{equation}
is $\frac{\gamma L}{2}$-averaged, and $\Fix T_G=\argmin h$. With the query
point $y_k=x_k$, the response $\widehat T_k(x_k)=x_k-\gamma u_k$ has the
error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-oracle}
 e_k=-\gamma\bigl(u_k-\nabla h(x_k)\bigr),\qquad b_k=0,\qquad
 \sigma_k\leq\gamma\tau_k.
\end{equation}
Hence (N1) holds; (N2) holds if $\sup_k\tau_k<\infty$; and (N6) holds if
$(s_kt_k\tau_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell^2_+(\mathscr F)$, respectively
$\ell^1_+(\mathscr F)$.

Substituting $\widehat T_k(x_k)=x_k-\gamma u_k$
into~\eqref{eq:stochastic-fast-km} and collecting terms, the stochastic
Fast KM method applied to $T_G$ reads
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-sgd}
 x_{k+1}=x_k+\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 -\frac{s\gamma(k+\alpha/2)}{k+\alpha}\,u_k
 +\frac{s\gamma k}{k+\alpha}\,u_{k-1},
 \qquad 0<s\leq\frac{2}{\gamma L}.
\end{equation}
Each iteration samples one gradient, at $x_k$; the vector $u_{k-1}$ is
the gradient sampled at $x_{k-1}$ and stored. The update is a momentum
step corrected by the difference of the current and stored sampled
gradients. For exact gradients, \cite[Theorems~3.4--3.5]{bot2023fast}
give $\norm{\nabla h(x_k)}=\gamma^{-1}\norm{x_k-T_Gx_k}=o(1/k)$ and weak
convergence of $x_k$ to a minimizer of $h$.

\subsection{Stochastic proximal gradient}\label{sec:app-fb}

Let $0<\gamma<2/L$. The forward--backward operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fb-map}
 T_{FB}:=\prox_{\gamma g}\circ(\Id-\gamma\nabla h)
\end{equation}
is $\frac{2}{4-\gamma L}$-averaged and $\Fix T_{FB}=\argmin(h+g)$; see
\cite[Section~4]{bot2023fast} with $C=\nabla h$ and $B=0$, or
\cite[Proposition~2.4]{combettes2015compositions}. With the query point
$y_k=x_k$, the oracle returns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fb-response}
 p_k:=\widehat T_k(x_k)=\prox_{\gamma g}\bigl(x_k-\gamma u_k\bigr),
\end{equation}
and the stochastic Fast KM method stores $p_{k-1}$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-fb}
 x_{k+1}=\Bigl(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\Bigr)x_k
 +\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\,p_k
 +\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}\,(p_k-p_{k-1}),
\end{equation}
with $0<s\leq2-\frac{\gamma L}{2}$. Each iteration samples one gradient
and evaluates one proximal map.

\begin{lemma}[Error of the forward--backward oracle]\label{lem:fb-error}
Under Assumptions~\ref{ass:composite} and~\ref{ass:sampled-gradient} with
$y_k=x_k$, the error $e_k=p_k-T_{FB}x_k$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fb-bias}
 \norm{e_k}\leq\gamma\norm{u_k-\nabla h(x_k)},\qquad
 \norm{b_k}\leq\gamma\tau_k,\qquad
 \sigma_k\leq\gamma\tau_k.
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The first bound follows from the nonexpansiveness of $\prox_{\gamma g}$.
By the conditional Jensen inequality,
$\norm{b_k}\leq\EE[\norm{e_k}\mid\FF_k]
\leq(\EE[\norm{e_k}^2\mid\FF_k])^{1/2}\leq\gamma\tau_k$. Finally,
$\sigma_k^2=\EE[\norm{e_k}^2\mid\FF_k]-\norm{b_k}^2\leq\gamma^2\tau_k^2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Unbiasedness of the full operator evaluation]\label{rem:prox}
An unbiased gradient passed through a nonlinear proximal map need not
produce an unbiased estimate of $T_{FB}x_k$. For example, let
$P(v)=\max\{v,0\}$ and let $w$ be an integrable, nonzero symmetric scalar
random variable. Then
\[
 \EE[P(-\gamma w)]=\frac{\gamma}{2}\EE\lvert w\rvert>0=P(0).
\]
Thus (N1) fails in general, and the bias is controlled only
through~\eqref{eq:fb-bias}.
\end{remark}

Lemma~\ref{lem:fb-error} shows that
$(s_kt_k\tau_k)_{k\geq0}\in\ell^1_+(\mathscr F)$ implies (N7) and both
forms of (N6). The bound $\norm{b_k}\leq\gamma\tau_k$ is of first order
in $\tau_k$, so the bias requires an $\ell^1$ condition. With
mini-batches $m_k\asymp k^q$ and $\tau_k^2=\tau^2/m_k$, this means $q>4$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-optimality}(i), the residual
$\norm{x_k-T_{FB}x_k}$ bounds
$\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial g(T_{FB}x_k)+\nabla h(T_{FB}x_k))$ up to
the factor $\gamma^{-1}+L$.

\subsection{Stochastic backward--forward splitting}\label{sec:app-bf}

Exchanging the order of the two steps gives the backward--forward operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf-map}
 T_{BF}:=(\Id-\gamma\nabla h)\circ\prox_{\gamma g},\qquad 0<\gamma<2/L,
\end{equation}
which is also $\frac{2}{4-\gamma L}$-averaged, since the averagedness
constant of a composition in \cite[Proposition~2.4]{combettes2015compositions}
is symmetric in the two factors. From
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-optimality}(ii) and the converse computation,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf-fix}
 \Fix T_{BF}=(\Id-\gamma\nabla h)\bigl(\argmin(h+g)\bigr),\qquad
 \prox_{\gamma g}(\Fix T_{BF})=\argmin(h+g).
\end{equation}
Indeed, if $p\in\argmin(h+g)$ and $x=p-\gamma\nabla h(p)$, then
$\gamma^{-1}(x-p)=-\nabla h(p)\in\partial g(p)$, so
$\prox_{\gamma g}(x)=p$ and $T_{BF}x=x$.

The proximal step is exact, so the query point $y_k=\prox_{\gamma g}(x_k)$
is $\FF_k$-measurable, and the response
$\widehat T_k(x_k)=y_k-\gamma u_k$ has the error
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bf-oracle}
 e_k=-\gamma\bigl(u_k-\nabla h(y_k)\bigr),\qquad b_k=0,\qquad
 \sigma_k\leq\gamma\tau_k.
\end{equation}
Thus (N1) holds, and only (N6) is required of the sampling scheme. This
ordering is the stochastic backward--forward splitting (SBF) of
\cite[Section~3.2 and Corollary~3.4]{cortild2026stochastic} and the
backward--forward reformulation used in \cite{trandinh2024vrfkm}. The
point of interest is the shadow $p_k:=\prox_{\gamma g}(x_k)$, for which
Lemma~\ref{lem:residual-optimality}(ii) gives
$\operatorname{dist}(0,\partial g(p_k)+\nabla h(p_k))
\leq\gamma^{-1}\norm{x_k-T_{BF}x_k}$. Weak convergence of $x_k$ does not
by itself give weak convergence of $p_k$, since $\prox_{\gamma g}$ is
not weakly continuous in general.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:app-summary}

Table~\ref{tab:applications} collects the three identifications. The
batch exponents refer to $m_k\asymp k^q$ and $\tau_k^2=\tau^2/m_k$.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{Stochastic first-order methods as instances of~\eqref{eq:oracle}.}
\label{tab:applications}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllll@{}}
\toprule
Method & $T$ & $\theta$ & Bias $b_k$ & Conditions; batch exponent \\
\midrule
SGD & $\Id-\gamma\nabla h$ & $\frac{\gamma L}{2}$ & $0$
 & (N1), (N6); $q>3$ for $\ell^2$, $q>4$ for $\ell^1$ \\
Prox.\ gradient & $\prox_{\gamma g}\circ(\Id-\gamma\nabla h)$
 & $\frac{2}{4-\gamma L}$ & $\norm{b_k}\leq\gamma\tau_k$
 & (N6), (N7); $q>4$ \\
Backward--forward & $(\Id-\gamma\nabla h)\circ\prox_{\gamma g}$
 & $\frac{2}{4-\gamma L}$ & $0$
 & (N1), (N6); $q>3$ for $\ell^2$, $q>4$ for $\ell^1$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The order of the proximal and gradient steps decides whether the oracle
is biased. In the forward--backward order the bias bound is of first
order in $\tau_k$, which forces an $\ell^1$ condition; the
backward--forward order keeps (N1) at the price of measuring optimality
at the shadow point $p_k$.
