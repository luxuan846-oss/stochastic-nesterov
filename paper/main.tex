\title{Stochastic Fast Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann Iteration\\
with Unbiased Oracle Evaluations}
\author{}
\date{Working draft --- October 15, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We consider the problem of finding a fixed point of a nonexpansive operator
in a real Hilbert space using conditionally unbiased stochastic oracle
evaluations. The operator and its fixed-point set are deterministic, whereas
the oracle responses and the iterates are random. We focus on a stochastic
implementation of the Fast Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann method that uses one new
oracle evaluation per iteration and retains the preceding evaluation.
The objective is to identify noise conditions under which the last-iterate
fixed-point residual decays at an accelerated almost-sure rate and the
iterates converge weakly almost surely. This initial draft specifies the
problem, the oracle model, and the iteration; stochastic convergence
theorems and sharp noise thresholds remain to be established.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Key words.} nonexpansive operator, fixed-point problem,
Fast KM, stochastic oracle, almost-sure convergence.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

We study the problem of finding a fixed point of a nonexpansive operator
$T:\HH\to\HH$ using noisy evaluations of $T$. Such problems arise from
convex optimization and operator splitting, where the fixed points of a
suitable update map encode solutions of the underlying problem
\cite{bot2023fast,yang2026ikm}. The map $T$ is fixed throughout the
iteration. Randomness enters through the computation of its values.
For instance, in smooth convex stochastic optimization, a sampled gradient
provides an unbiased estimate of the gradient of a deterministic expectation
objective \cite[Section~4.3]{bravo2024stochastic}.

\subsection{Background and motivation}\label{sec:motivation}

The Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann (KM) iteration is a basic method for
nonexpansive fixed-point problems. Bo\c{t} and Nguyen
\cite[Algorithm~2.1 and Theorems~3.4--3.5]{bot2023fast} proposed the
Fast KM method, which combines inertial and operator-difference terms.
Under their parameter conditions, the deterministic method satisfies
$\norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(1/k)$ and its iterates converge weakly to a fixed point.
These guarantees motivate the study of the same iteration with stochastic
oracle evaluations.

Replacing exact evaluations by noisy responses introduces a specific
dependence on past errors. In the implementation considered here, the
previous oracle response is stored and used again in the next update.
Consequently, the update contains both the current error and the preceding
error, with opposite signs and different coefficients. The preceding error
is already known before the current query, so conditional unbiasedness
does not remove all error terms from a one-step energy estimate. The
question is how this dependence affects the noise conditions needed for
almost-sure residual rates and convergence of the iterates.

\subsection{Related work}\label{sec:related-work}

Bravo and Cominetti \cite{bravo2024stochastic} analyze stochastic KM
iterations in finite-dimensional normed spaces, including almost-sure
convergence and bounds on the expected fixed-point residual.
Yang et al.\ \cite[Theorem~1]{yang2026ikm} study stochastic inertial KM
with one oracle response per update. Their parameter schedule depends on
a prescribed horizon, and their main result controls the root-mean-square
residual at the last iterate. Their oracle model allows a predictable
bias; we restrict attention to its conditionally unbiased case.
Almost-sure accelerated residual rates are also available for other
inexact fixed-point methods, including the AFP framework of
Nguyen-Trung and Tran-Dinh \cite[Theorem~2]{nguyentrung2025afp}, under
the assumptions of that theorem. Thus, an almost-sure accelerated rate
alone does not distinguish the present research question.

Our focus is the stochastic Fast KM recurrence specified in
Section~\ref{sec:algorithm}, run indefinitely with parameters independent
of a terminal horizon. We seek noise conditions for the deterministic
residual rate to persist almost surely, together with weak convergence of
the iterates. Comparing those conditions across noise distributions and
query implementations is a further objective. No sharp threshold or
novelty claim is asserted in this draft.

\section{Problem formulation and stochastic oracle}\label{sec:problem}

\subsection{Nonexpansive fixed-point problem}\label{sec:fixed-point}

Let $\HH$ be a real separable Hilbert space with inner product
$\ip{\cdot}{\cdot}$ and induced norm $\norm{\cdot}$.

\begin{assumption}[Operator and solution set]\label{ass:operator}
The deterministic operator $T:\HH\to\HH$ is nonexpansive:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:nonexpansive}
 \norm{Tx-Ty}\leq\norm{x-y},\qquad x,y\in\HH.
\end{equation}
Its fixed-point set $\Fix T:=\{x\in\HH:Tx=x\}$ is nonempty.
\end{assumption}

The problem is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:problem}
 \text{find }x^\star\in\HH\quad\text{such that}\quad Tx^\star=x^\star.
\end{equation}
We measure accuracy by the fixed-point residual
\begin{equation}\label{eq:residual}
 r(x):=\norm{x-Tx},\qquad r_k:=r(x_k).
\end{equation}
For $V:=\Id-T$, problem~\eqref{eq:problem} is equivalently $Vx^\star=0$.
Nonexpansiveness of $T$ is equivalent to $1/2$-cocoercivity of this $V$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:cocoercivity}
 \ip{Vx-Vy}{x-y}\geq\tfrac12\norm{Vx-Vy}^{2},\qquad x,y\in\HH.
\end{equation}
Indeed, expanding $\norm{(x-y)-(Vx-Vy)}^2\leq\norm{x-y}^2$
gives~\eqref{eq:cocoercivity}, and the expansion is reversible.
The residual does not by itself provide a bound on the distance to
$\Fix T$ without an additional error-bound condition.

\subsection{Conditionally unbiased oracle}\label{sec:oracle}

Work on a probability space $(\Omega,\FF,\PP)$ equipped with a
filtration $(\FF_k)_{k\geq0}$. The sigma-algebra $\FF_k$ contains all
information available immediately before query $k$. At an
$\FF_k$-measurable point $x_k$, the oracle returns
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle}
 \widehat T_k(x_k)=Tx_k+e_k.
\end{equation}
All Hilbert-space-valued random variables are strongly measurable.
The error $e_k$ is square-integrable and $\FF_{k+1}$-measurable.
We impose conditional unbiasedness:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:unbiased}
 \EE[e_k\mid\FF_k]=0\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
This is the specialization $b_k=0$ of the oracle model in
\cite[Section~2.2, equation~(6)]{yang2026ikm}. Independence of errors
across iterations is not assumed.

Let $(\sigma_k^2)_{k\geq0}$ be a deterministic sequence of finite,
nonnegative numbers satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:variance-budget}
 \EE[\norm{e_k}^2\mid\FF_k]\leq\sigma_k^2
 \qquad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
We distinguish two noise conditions used in the research plan:
\begin{description}
 \item[\textnormal{(N2)}] Uniformly bounded conditional variance:
 $\sup_{k\geq0}\sigma_k^2<\infty$.
 \item[\textnormal{(N6)}] Weighted square summability, specialized here to
 fixed-step Fast KM:
 \begin{equation}\label{eq:n6}
  \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(k+1)^2\sigma_k^2<\infty.
 \end{equation}
\end{description}
The shift by one includes the initialization error and does not change
the asymptotic summability requirement. We use (N6) as a candidate
sufficient condition to investigate, not as an established convergence
theorem for the recurrence below. A bounded variance budget in (N2)
alone does not require the errors to vanish.

\begin{remark}[The target is deterministic]\label{rem:target}
The operator $T$ and the set $\Fix T$ are deterministic. The oracle
responses and the iterates are random. A limit selected by the iteration
may consequently be a random variable with values in $\Fix T$.
The model does not require the sampled mappings to have a common fixed
point or to be individually nonexpansive.
\end{remark}

\subsection{A stochastic optimization example}\label{sec:example}

Let $f(x)=\EE_{\zeta}[F(x,\zeta)]$ be convex and differentiable,
with $L$-Lipschitz gradient, $L>0$, and a nonempty set of minimizers.
For $0<\gamma\leq2/L$, the operator
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-map}
 Tx=x-\gamma\nabla f(x)
\end{equation}
is nonexpansive and $\Fix T=\argmin f$.
Assume that a sampled gradient $g_k$ at $x_k$ satisfies
$\EE[g_k\mid\FF_k]=\nabla f(x_k)$ and has finite conditional
second moment. This unbiasedness must be justified by the sampling
model and, when using $\nabla_xF$, the interchange of differentiation
and expectation. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gradient-oracle}
 \widehat T_k(x_k)=x_k-\gamma g_k,
 \qquad e_k=-\gamma\bigl(g_k-\nabla f(x_k)\bigr)
\end{equation}
satisfies~\eqref{eq:unbiased}. This is the stochastic-gradient
interpretation of KM described in
\cite[Section~4.3]{bravo2024stochastic}.

\begin{remark}[Unbiasedness of the full operator evaluation]\label{rem:prox}
An unbiased gradient passed through a nonlinear proximal map need not
produce an unbiased estimate of the resulting fixed-point operator.
For example, let $P(u)=\max\{u,0\}$ and let $\xi$ be an integrable,
nonzero symmetric scalar noise. Then
\[
 \EE[P(-\gamma\xi)]=\frac{\gamma}{2}\EE|\xi|>0=P(0).
\]
Thus, for a proximal-gradient map, condition~\eqref{eq:unbiased}
must be checked for the complete oracle response. The existence of a
deterministic splitting formulation alone does not verify this condition.
\end{remark}

\section{Fast KM iteration}\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{The Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann iteration}\label{sec:km}

For the fixed-point problem~\eqref{eq:problem}, the Krasnosel'ski\u{i}--Mann
(KM) iteration takes the form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:km}
 x_{k+1}=(1-\lambda_k)x_k+\lambda_k Tx_k,\qquad k\geq0,
\end{equation}
where $x_0\in\HH$ and $\lambda_k\in(0,1)$ is a relaxation parameter.
Each update is a convex combination of the current iterate and its image
under $T$. Under Assumption~\ref{ass:operator}, for a fixed relaxation
parameter $\lambda_k\equiv\lambda\in(0,1)$, the iterates converge weakly
to a point in $\Fix T$ and the fixed-point residual satisfies
$\norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(k^{-1/2})$; see
\cite[Section~1, equation~(1.3)]{bot2023fast}.

\subsection{The Fast KM method}\label{sec:deterministic-fast-km}

Bo\c{t} and Nguyen \cite{bot2023fast} accelerate the KM iteration using
Nesterov's momentum updates. For fixed parameters $\alpha>2$ and
$0<s\leq1$, and initial points $x_0,x_1\in\HH$, their Fast KM method is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deterministic-fast-km}
\begin{split}
 x_{k+1}={}&\left(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}Tx_k\\
 &+\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}),\qquad k\geq1.
\end{split}
\end{equation}
This is equation~(2.7) in \cite{bot2023fast}, specialized to a
nonexpansive operator. The update involves both the difference of successive
iterates and the difference of successive operator values.
Under Assumption~\ref{ass:operator},
\cite[Theorems~3.4--3.5]{bot2023fast} give
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deterministic-guarantees}
 \norm{x_k-Tx_k}=o(1/k),\qquad
 x_k\rightharpoonup x^\star\in\Fix T.
\end{equation}

For $s=1$, the coefficient of $x_k-x_{k-1}$ in
\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km} vanishes, and the iteration becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:fast-km-unit-step}
 x_{k+1}=\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}Tx_k
 +\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}).
\end{equation}
As observed in \cite[Remark~2.1]{bot2023fast}, this is a KM iteration
with the additional extrapolation term
$\frac{k}{k+\alpha}(Tx_k-Tx_{k-1})$. The extrapolation acts on the
operator values. For $0<s<1$, the explicit inertial term
$\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})$ is also present.

\subsection{Stochastic Fast KM}\label{sec:iteration}

We replace the exact evaluations in~\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km}
by the conditionally unbiased oracle responses from Section~\ref{sec:oracle}.
Choose deterministic $x_0,x_1\in\HH$ and fixed $\alpha>2$, $0<s\leq1$.
Initialize by computing and storing $\widehat T_0(x_0)$. For every $k\geq1$,
compute $\widehat T_k(x_k)$ and set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:stochastic-fast-km}
\begin{split}
 x_{k+1}={}&\left(1-\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\right)x_k
 +\frac{(1-s)k}{k+\alpha}(x_k-x_{k-1})
 +\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}\widehat T_k(x_k)\\
 &+\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}
 \bigl(\widehat T_k(x_k)-\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})\bigr).
\end{split}
\end{equation}
The preceding response $\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})$ is stored from the
previous iteration. Hence, after initialization, each update requires one
new oracle evaluation. The points $x_{k-1}$ and $x_k$, together with the
stored response, are $\FF_k$-measurable; $x_{k+1}$ is
$\FF_{k+1}$-measurable. The parameters are independent of a terminal horizon.

Substituting~\eqref{eq:oracle} into the difference of oracle responses yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:oracle-difference}
 \widehat T_k(x_k)-\widehat T_{k-1}(x_{k-1})
 =Tx_k-Tx_{k-1}+e_k-e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Relative to the exact update~\eqref{eq:deterministic-fast-km} evaluated
at the current iterates, the total additive error is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:perturbed-recurrence}
 \eta_k=\frac{s\alpha}{2(k+\alpha)}e_k
       +\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}(e_k-e_{k-1})
       =\frac{s(k+\alpha/2)}{k+\alpha}e_k
       -\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
Since $e_{k-1}$ is $\FF_k$-measurable, conditional unbiasedness gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:conditional-update-error}
 \EE[\eta_k\mid\FF_k]=-\frac{sk}{k+\alpha}e_{k-1}.
\end{equation}
These identities describe how the current and preceding oracle errors
enter the stochastic iteration.

Our objective is to identify noise conditions under which
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-rate}
 k\norm{x_k-Tx_k}\longrightarrow0\qquad\text{almost surely},
\end{equation}
and, for a $\Fix T$-valued random variable $x_\infty$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:target-iterates}
 x_k\rightharpoonup x_\infty\qquad\text{almost surely}.
\end{equation}
